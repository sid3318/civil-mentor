## 1. Quick anchor

The 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 gave panchayats constitutional status. It added Part IX (Articles 243 to 243-O) and the Eleventh Schedule, which lists 29 subjects that states may devolve to panchayats. It came into force on 24 April 1993, now observed as National Panchayati Raj Day.

## 2. How it got there

| Year | Committee or event | What it proposed |
|---|---|---|
| 1957 | Balwantrai Mehta Committee | Three-tier "democratic decentralisation". Rajasthan was the first state to adopt it, at Nagaur in 1959 |
| 1978 | Ashok Mehta Committee | Two tiers, with the district as the base of decentralisation |
| 1986 | L.M. Singhvi Committee | Constitutional recognition for local self-government |

## 3. What the amendment requires

These provisions are compulsory for every state:

- A **Gram Sabha** of all registered voters in a village (Article 243A).
- **Three tiers**: village, intermediate and district. A state with a population under 20 lakh may leave out the intermediate tier.
- **Direct elections** to seats at all three levels.
- **Reservation** of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to their population, and of not less than one-third of all seats for women (Article 243D). Many states have raised the share for women to one-half.
- A **five-year term**, with fresh elections within six months if a panchayat is dissolved early.
- A **State Election Commission** to conduct the elections (Article 243K).
- A **State Finance Commission**, set up by the Governor every five years, to review panchayat finances (Article 243-I).

These are left to the states:

- Reservation for backward classes.
- Which of the 29 subjects to devolve, and with what money and staff.

## 4. The 74th Amendment and PESA

- The 74th Amendment Act, 1992 did the same for towns and cities: Part IXA (Articles 243P to 243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule with 18 subjects.
- The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996, known as PESA, extends Part IX to Fifth Schedule areas and gives the Gram Sabha powers over minor forest produce, land alienation and local resources.

## 5. What still holds panchayats back

Funds, functions and functionaries: states have devolved subjects on paper without the money or staff to run them. Panchayats raise little revenue of their own and depend on grants.

## The trap

The one-third reservation for women applies to seats and to the offices of chairpersons at every level. Reservation for backward classes is not compulsory. The minimum age to contest a panchayat election is 21, not 25.

- 73rd Amendment 1992: Part IX, Articles 243 to 243-O, Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects. In force 24 April 1993.
- Balwantrai Mehta 1957 (three tiers, Nagaur 1959), Ashok Mehta 1978 (two tiers), L.M. Singhvi 1986 (constitutional status).
- Compulsory: Gram Sabha, three tiers (two if under 20 lakh people), direct elections, SC/ST and one-third women's reservation, five-year term, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission.
- Voluntary: backward-class reservation and what to devolve.
- 74th Amendment: municipalities, Part IXA, Twelfth Schedule with 18 subjects.
- PESA 1996 extends panchayats to Fifth Schedule areas with a stronger Gram Sabha.
- Minimum age to contest: 21.
