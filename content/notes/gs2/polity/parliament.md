## 1. Quick anchor

Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). The President is part of Parliament without sitting in either House, because no bill becomes law without the President's assent.

## 2. The two Houses

| | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum strength | 550 elected members | 250: 238 elected and 12 nominated |
| How chosen | Directly, by voters in territorial constituencies | Indirectly, by elected members of state and some union territory legislatures, by single transferable vote |
| Term | Five years, unless dissolved earlier | Permanent. One-third of members retire every second year, each serving six years |
| Presiding officer | Speaker, elected by the House | Vice-President, as ex officio Chairman |

The 104th Amendment Act, 2019 ended the nomination of two Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha.

## 3. Kinds of bills

1. **Ordinary bills** can start in either House. A deadlock can be resolved at a joint sitting under Article 108, presided over by the Speaker.
2. **Money Bills** (Article 110) deal only with taxes, borrowing and the Consolidated Fund. They start only in the Lok Sabha, on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes and must return the bill within 14 days. The Speaker's decision that a bill is a Money Bill is final.
3. **Financial bills** (Article 117) contain money matters along with other provisions, and are otherwise treated like ordinary bills.
4. **Constitution amendment bills** (Article 368) need a special majority in each House separately. There is no joint sitting for them.

Joint sittings have been held three times: in 1961, 1978 and 2002.

## 4. Sessions

- The President summons and prorogues the Houses and can dissolve the Lok Sabha.
- Article 85: no more than six months may pass between two sessions.
- By convention there are three sessions a year: Budget, Monsoon and Winter.

## 5. Tools of accountability

- **Question Hour**, normally the first hour of a sitting.
- **Zero Hour**, which follows it. It is an Indian practice and is not mentioned in the rules.
- **Adjournment motion**, to discuss an urgent matter of public importance. Only in the Lok Sabha.
- **No-confidence motion**, admitted with the support of at least 50 members. Only in the Lok Sabha, since the Council of Ministers is responsible to that House.

## 6. Financial committees

| Committee | Members | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Public Accounts Committee | 22 (15 Lok Sabha, 7 Rajya Sabha) | Examines the audit reports of the CAG. Chaired by an opposition member by convention |
| Estimates Committee | 30, all from the Lok Sabha | Suggests economies in the budget estimates |
| Committee on Public Undertakings | 22 (15 Lok Sabha, 7 Rajya Sabha) | Examines public sector undertakings |

## The trap

The Rajya Sabha can discuss a Money Bill but cannot amend or reject it. The Estimates Committee has no Rajya Sabha members. Neither Money Bills nor amendment bills can go to a joint sitting.

<!-- distilled -->

- Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha (Article 79).
- Lok Sabha: at most 550 elected, five-year term. Rajya Sabha: at most 250 (238 elected, 12 nominated), permanent, one-third retire every two years.
- Money Bill (Article 110): Lok Sabha only, President's recommendation, Rajya Sabha has 14 days and can only recommend. Speaker's word is final.
- Joint sitting (Article 108) for ordinary bills only, presided over by the Speaker. Held in 1961, 1978 and 2002.
- Amendment bills (Article 368): special majority in each House, no joint sitting.
- At most six months between sessions (Article 85).
- Adjournment and no-confidence motions: Lok Sabha only. No-confidence needs 50 members.
- PAC 22 and Estimates 30 (all Lok Sabha). COPU 22.
