## 1. Quick anchor

Indian temple architecture is described in three styles: Nagara in the north, Dravida in the south, and Vesara, a blend of the two, in the Deccan. All three share one plan: a dark inner sanctum holding the deity, approached through halls, with a tower rising above the sanctum.

## 2. The parts of a temple

- **Garbhagriha**: the sanctum, a small dark room that holds the main image.
- **Mandapa**: the hall where worshippers gather.
- **Antarala**: the vestibule joining the mandapa to the sanctum.
- **Shikhara or vimana**: the tower over the sanctum. North Indian texts say shikhara; south Indian texts say vimana.
- **Pradakshina patha**: the path for walking around the sanctum.

## 3. Nagara and Dravida compared

| Feature | Nagara (north) | Dravida (south) |
|---|---|---|
| Tower | Curved shikhara that rises in a single sweep, crowned by an amalaka and a kalasha | Stepped, pyramid-like vimana |
| Gateways | Usually no large gateways | Gopurams, which in later temples grow taller than the vimana |
| Enclosure | Usually no boundary wall | A walled courtyard, often with a temple tank |
| Base | Often a raised stone platform | Built at ground level within the enclosure |

## 4. Schools and examples

**Nagara.** In Odisha, the Lingaraja temple at Bhubaneswar and the Sun Temple at Konark, built under the Eastern Gangas in the thirteenth century. At Khajuraho, the Kandariya Mahadeva temple of the Chandelas. In Gujarat, the Sun Temple at Modhera, built under the Solankis.

**Dravida.** The Pallavas built the Shore Temple at Mamallapuram and the Kailasanatha temple at Kanchipuram. The Cholas built the Brihadeeswara temple at Thanjavur, completed under Rajaraja I around 1010. Later dynasties added the great gopurams, as at the Meenakshi temple in Madurai.

**Vesara.** The Chalukyas of Badami experimented at Aihole and Pattadakal. Pattadakal has temples in both the Nagara and Dravida styles side by side. The Hoysalas built the temples at Belur and Halebidu in soapstone, on star-shaped platforms, with dense carving.

## 5. Panchayatana

In the panchayatana plan the main shrine stands at the centre of a platform with four smaller shrines at the corners. The Dashavatara temple at Deogarh is the usual example.

## The trap

The gopuram, not the vimana, is the tallest structure in many later south Indian temples. Do not use height to identify the main shrine. Pattadakal is the site that shows both northern and southern styles together.

<!-- distilled -->

- Three styles: Nagara (north), Dravida (south), Vesara (Deccan blend).
- Parts: garbhagriha, mandapa, antarala, shikhara or vimana, pradakshina patha.
- Nagara: curved shikhara with amalaka and kalasha, no big gateways or walls.
- Dravida: stepped vimana, gopurams, walled courtyard, tank.
- Nagara examples: Lingaraja, Konark, Kandariya Mahadeva, Modhera.
- Dravida examples: Shore Temple, Kailasanatha (Kanchipuram), Brihadeeswara (Rajaraja I), Meenakshi.
- Vesara: Chalukya Aihole and Pattadakal, Hoysala Belur and Halebidu on star-shaped platforms.
- Panchayatana: a central shrine with four corner shrines, as at Deogarh.
