*Sample note. Written to show the format, not as finished study material.*

## 1. Quick anchor

Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915. Within five years he had turned the Congress from a party of petitions into a mass movement, using satyagraha: non-violent refusal to obey an unjust law, with the willingness to accept the penalty. The national movement from 1917 to 1947 moved in waves of mass action separated by periods of constructive work.

## 2. The first local satyagrahas

| Year | Where | The issue |
|---|---|---|
| 1917 | Champaran, Bihar | Indigo cultivators forced to grow indigo on part of their land (the tinkathia system) |
| 1918 | Ahmedabad | A wage dispute in the cotton mills. Gandhi used a hunger strike for the first time in India |
| 1918 | Kheda, Gujarat | Peasants asking for land revenue to be remitted after the crops failed |

These made Gandhi a national figure before any all-India campaign began.

## 3. The three mass movements

**Non-Cooperation (1920 to 1922).** Launched after the Rowlatt Act and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 13 April 1919, and joined to the Khilafat cause. Indians returned titles and boycotted councils, courts, schools and foreign cloth. Gandhi withdrew it in February 1922 after a crowd burnt a police station at Chauri Chaura.

**Civil Disobedience (1930 to 1934).** The Lahore session of December 1929 adopted Purna Swaraj, complete independence, as the goal. Gandhi began the Dandi March on 12 March 1930 and broke the salt law at Dandi on 6 April. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931 paused the movement so that Gandhi could attend the Second Round Table Conference in London.

**Quit India (1942).** The Congress passed the Quit India resolution in Bombay on 8 August 1942. Gandhi's call was "Do or die". The leaders were arrested the next morning, and the movement was carried on by underground networks and parallel governments in places such as Satara and Ballia.

## 4. The Poona Pact, 1932

The Communal Award of August 1932 gave the depressed classes separate electorates. Gandhi fasted in Yerawada jail against it. The Poona Pact between Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar replaced separate electorates with a larger number of seats reserved for the depressed classes, elected through joint electorates.

## 5. Towards independence

- The Cabinet Mission of 1946 proposed a federal union and a Constituent Assembly.
- The Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 accepted partition.
- The Indian Independence Act, 1947 created two dominions, India and Pakistan, from 15 August 1947.

## The trap

Chronology questions mix these events freely. Fix four anchors and place everything else around them: Champaran 1917, Non-Cooperation 1920, Dandi 1930, Quit India 1942. The Poona Pact withdrew separate electorates; it did not grant them.

<!-- distilled -->

- Satyagraha: non-violent refusal to obey an unjust law, accepting the penalty.
- Local satyagrahas: Champaran 1917 (indigo), Ahmedabad 1918 (mill wages), Kheda 1918 (revenue).
- Non-Cooperation 1920 to 1922, with Khilafat. Withdrawn after Chauri Chaura, February 1922.
- Lahore 1929: Purna Swaraj. Dandi March from 12 March 1930, salt law broken on 6 April.
- Gandhi-Irwin Pact, March 1931, then the Second Round Table Conference.
- Poona Pact 1932: reserved seats in joint electorates in place of the Communal Award's separate electorates.
- Quit India resolution, 8 August 1942: "Do or die".
- Mountbatten Plan, 3 June 1947. Independence on 15 August 1947.
