## 1. Quick anchor

The monsoon is a seasonal reversal of winds. From June to September, moist south-westerly winds blow from the sea to the land and bring about three-quarters of India's annual rain. In winter the winds reverse and blow from the land to the sea.

## 2. Why the winds reverse

- **Differential heating.** In summer the land heats faster than the sea, and a low-pressure area forms over north-west India and the Tibetan Plateau.
- **The ITCZ shifts north.** The Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone moves to about 25°N over the Ganga plain in July. In this position it is called the monsoon trough.
- **Winds cross the equator.** South-east trade winds from the southern Indian Ocean cross the equator, are turned right by the Coriolis force, and arrive as south-westerlies.
- **Jet streams.** The subtropical westerly jet withdraws north of the Himalaya, and the tropical easterly jet sets in over the peninsula.

## 3. Onset, spread and withdrawal

1. The monsoon normally reaches Kerala around 1 June.
2. It splits into two branches: the Arabian Sea branch and the Bay of Bengal branch.
3. It covers the whole country by about 8 July.
4. Withdrawal begins from north-west India around the middle of September.

A "break" in the monsoon is a dry spell of a week or more in July or August, when the trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills.

## 4. Who gets how much rain

- The windward side of the Western Ghats and the north-east get very heavy rain.
- The leeward side of the Ghats lies in a rain shadow.
- The Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu stays largely dry in the south-west monsoon. It gets most of its rain from the north-east monsoon, from October to December.

## 5. What makes a good or bad year

| Factor | Good for the monsoon | Bad for the monsoon |
|---|---|---|
| ENSO | La Niña | El Niño |
| Indian Ocean Dipole | Positive phase | Negative phase |
| Madden-Julian Oscillation | Active phase over the Indian Ocean | Suppressed phase |

The India Meteorological Department calls the season normal when rainfall is between 96 and 104 per cent of the Long Period Average.

## The trap

El Niño raises the odds of a weak monsoon. It does not guarantee one. A positive Indian Ocean Dipole can offset it, as in 1997.

- Seasonal wind reversal: sea to land in summer, land to sea in winter. June to September brings about 75 per cent of the rain.
- Causes: land heats faster, the ITCZ shifts to about 25°N, trades cross the equator and turn south-westerly, jets rearrange.
- Kerala onset about 1 June. Whole country by about 8 July. Withdrawal from mid-September.
- Two branches: Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.

- La Niña and a positive Indian Ocean Dipole help. El Niño hurts but is not decisive.
