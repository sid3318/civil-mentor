## 1. Quick anchor

Biodiversity is the variety of life at three levels: genes, species and ecosystems. Conservation is done in two ways. In situ conservation protects species where they live, in national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves. Ex situ conservation protects them outside their habitat, in zoos, botanical gardens and seed banks.

## 2. Biodiversity hotspots

A hotspot is a region with at least 1,500 species of vascular plants found nowhere else that has lost at least 70 per cent of its original habitat. Norman Myers proposed the idea in 1988, and Conservation International now maintains the list.

India has parts of four hotspots:

1. The Himalaya
2. Indo-Burma, which covers most of north-east India
3. The Western Ghats and Sri Lanka
4. Sundaland, which includes the Nicobar Islands

## 3. Protected areas in India

The Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 provides four kinds of protected area.

| Kind | What sets it apart |
|---|---|
| National park | The strictest. No grazing and no private rights inside it |
| Wildlife sanctuary | Some activities, such as grazing, can be allowed |
| Conservation reserve | Government land, often a corridor between parks, managed with local people |
| Community reserve | Community or private land that its owners choose to protect |

Biosphere reserves are not created under this Act. They come from UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere programme. Each has a core zone left undisturbed, a buffer zone for research and education, and a transition zone where people live and work. The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, set up in 1986, was India's first.

## 4. The international treaties

| Treaty | Year | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Ramsar Convention | 1971 | Wetlands of international importance and their wise use. India joined in 1982 |
| CITES | 1973 | International trade in endangered species, listed in three appendices |
| Convention on Migratory Species (Bonn) | 1979 | Animals that cross national borders |
| Convention on Biological Diversity | 1992 | Conservation, sustainable use and fair sharing of benefits from genetic resources |

The Convention on Biological Diversity has two protocols: Cartagena (2000) on the safe movement of living modified organisms, and Nagoya (2010) on access to genetic resources and benefit sharing. The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework of 2022 set the "30 by 30" target: protect 30 per cent of land and sea by 2030.

## 5. India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002

It puts the Convention into Indian law through three tiers: the National Biodiversity Authority in Chennai, a State Biodiversity Board in each state, and Biodiversity Management Committees in local bodies, which keep People's Biodiversity Registers.

## The trap

Sundaland looks like a purely South-East Asian hotspot, but the Nicobar Islands put India in it. Ramsar protects wetlands, not birds, even though many Ramsar sites are known for birds. Biosphere reserves have no basis in the Wild Life (Protection) Act.

- Ramsar 1971 (wetlands; India 1982), CITES 1973 (trade), CMS 1979 (migratory species), CBD 1992.

- Biological Diversity Act 2002: NBA (Chennai), State Biodiversity Boards, Biodiversity Management Committees.
