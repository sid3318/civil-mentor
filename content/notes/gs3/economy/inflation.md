## 1. Quick anchor

Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. India measures it mainly through the Consumer Price Index (CPI), and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is legally bound to keep CPI inflation near a target set by the government.

## 2. How inflation is measured

| Index | Published by | What it tracks |
|---|---|---|
| CPI (Combined) | National Statistics Office | Retail prices of goods and services bought by households |
| WPI | Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT | Wholesale prices of goods only |

- **Headline inflation** is the change in the whole index.
- **Core inflation** leaves out food and fuel, whose prices swing the most.

## 3. Why prices rise

- **Demand-pull**: demand grows faster than supply. Causes include higher government spending, tax cuts and easy credit.
- **Cost-push**: the cost of producing goods rises. Causes include costlier crude oil, a weaker rupee and crop failures.

## 4. The inflation target

The RBI Act was amended in 2016 to create a flexible inflation-targeting framework.

- The Government of India sets the target, in consultation with the RBI, once every five years.
- Since 2016 the target has been 4 per cent CPI inflation, with a tolerance band of 2 percentage points on either side.
- If inflation stays outside the band for three consecutive quarters, the RBI must explain the failure to the government in writing.

## 5. The Monetary Policy Committee

- Six members: three from the RBI, including the Governor, and three appointed by the central government.
- The Governor chairs it and has a casting vote when votes are tied.
- It must meet at least four times a year and it decides the repo rate.

## 6. The RBI's tools

1. **Repo rate**: the rate at which the RBI lends to banks against government securities.
2. **Standing Deposit Facility**: the floor of the rate corridor since April 2022. Banks park money with the RBI without collateral.
3. **Marginal Standing Facility**: the ceiling of the corridor, for emergency overnight borrowing.
4. **Cash Reserve Ratio**: the share of deposits banks keep as cash with the RBI.
5. **Statutory Liquidity Ratio**: the share banks hold themselves in cash, gold or approved securities.
6. **Open market operations**: buying and selling government securities to add or remove liquidity.

## The trap

The WPI has no services in it. A question that says "WPI captures the price of services" is wrong. The inflation target is set by the government, not by the RBI or the committee.

<!-- distilled -->

- CPI is published by the NSO and covers goods and services. WPI is published by DPIIT's Economic Adviser and covers goods only.
- Core inflation leaves out food and fuel.
- Demand-pull comes from spending and credit. Cost-push comes from input costs such as crude oil.
- Target: 4 per cent CPI, plus or minus 2. Set by the government with the RBI, every five years.
- The committee has six members, three from the RBI and three from the government. The Governor has the casting vote.
- Failure means three consecutive quarters outside the band.
- The corridor runs from the Standing Deposit Facility (floor) through the repo rate to the Marginal Standing Facility (ceiling).
