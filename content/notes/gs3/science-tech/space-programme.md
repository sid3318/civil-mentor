## 1. Quick anchor

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) was founded in 1969 and works under the Department of Space, set up in 1972. Its programme began with satellites for communication, weather and remote sensing, and has grown to planetary missions and a human spaceflight programme.

## 2. Orbits

| Orbit | Height | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Low Earth orbit | Up to about 2,000 km | Space stations, Earth observation |
| Sun-synchronous polar orbit | About 600 to 800 km | Remote sensing. The satellite passes over each place at the same local time |
| Medium Earth orbit | About 20,200 km for GPS | Global navigation |
| Geostationary orbit | About 35,786 km above the equator | Communication and weather. The satellite takes one sidereal day to go round, so it stays over the same point |

## 3. Launch vehicles

- **PSLV**, the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle, has four stages that alternate solid and liquid fuel. It launched Chandrayaan-1, the Mars Orbiter Mission and, in February 2017, 104 satellites in one flight.
- **GSLV** uses an indigenous cryogenic upper stage to reach geosynchronous transfer orbit.
- **LVM3**, formerly GSLV Mark III, is the heaviest. It launched Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3 and is the rocket chosen for Gaganyaan.
- **SSLV**, the Small Satellite Launch Vehicle, is for small satellites on short notice. Its first successful flight was in February 2023.

## 4. Missions to know

1. **Chandrayaan-1** (2008) carried instruments that found evidence of water molecules on the Moon. Its Moon Impact Probe struck at a site named Jawahar Point.
2. **Mars Orbiter Mission** (launched 2013, in Mars orbit from September 2014). India was the first Asian country to reach Mars orbit, and the first country to do so on its first attempt.
3. **Chandrayaan-2** (2019). The orbiter still works. The Vikram lander crashed, at the site now called Tiranga Point.
4. **Chandrayaan-3** landed near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023, the first landing in that region. The landing site is Shiv Shakti Point, and 23 August is observed as National Space Day.
5. **Aditya-L1** (2023) is India's first solar observatory in space. It orbits the Lagrange point L1, about 1.5 million km from Earth towards the Sun.

## 5. Navigation and the private sector

- **NavIC** is India's regional navigation system. It covers India and about 1,500 km beyond its borders, from satellites in geostationary and geosynchronous orbits.
- **IN-SPACe** (2020) authorises and promotes private space companies.
- **NewSpace India Limited** (2019) is ISRO's commercial arm.

## The trap

A geostationary satellite is about 36,000 km up, not 20,000 km: that is the navigation orbit. Remote-sensing satellites go into polar, sun-synchronous orbits, not geostationary ones. Keep the three lunar points apart: Jawahar (Chandrayaan-1), Tiranga (Chandrayaan-2) and Shiv Shakti (Chandrayaan-3).
