## 1. Quick anchor

Probity is integrity and uprightness in public life: doing what is right even where the law is silent or would not catch the wrong. It goes beyond legality. A decision can be legal and still lack probity, for example when an officer awards a contract to a relative through a correct procedure.

## 2. The Nolan principles

The Committee on Standards in Public Life in the United Kingdom, chaired by Lord Nolan, set out seven principles of public life in 1995:

1. Selflessness
2. Integrity
3. Objectivity
4. Accountability
5. Openness
6. Honesty
7. Leadership

They are the standard list to quote, and India's Second Administrative Reforms Commission drew on them.

## 3. India's framework

| Instrument | What it does |
|---|---|
| Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 | Defines corruption offences by public servants. The 2018 amendment made giving a bribe an offence and requires prior approval before a public servant is investigated for official decisions |
| Central Vigilance Commission | Set up in 1964 on the Santhanam Committee's advice. Statutory since the CVC Act, 2003 |
| Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 | An anti-corruption ombudsman at the Centre and in the states |
| Right to Information Act, 2005 | Gives citizens the right to see government records |
| Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 | Protects people who expose corruption |
| All India Services (Conduct) Rules, 1968 | The conduct code for IAS, IPS and IFoS officers |

## 4. What the Second ARC said

Its fourth report, *Ethics in Governance* (2007), recommended a code of ethics for ministers, legislators and civil servants, laws against conflicts of interest, and the confiscation of property gained through corruption.

## 5. Threats to probity

- **Conflict of interest**: a private interest that could sway an official decision.
- **Nepotism and favouritism** in appointments and contracts.
- **Policy capture**: rules shaped for a narrow group rather than the public.
- **A culture of tolerance**, where small favours are seen as normal.

## 6. What strengthens it

Transparency through e-governance and open data, social audits such as those under MGNREGA, citizen's charters, protection for whistle-blowers, and the personal example of senior officers.

## The trap

Probity is not the same as legality, and it is not only about money. Misuse of time, office or information is also a breach. In case studies, show the step beyond what the rules require.
