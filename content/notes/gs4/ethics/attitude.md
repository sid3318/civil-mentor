## 1. Quick anchor

An attitude is a learned and fairly lasting evaluation of a person, group, object or idea: favourable or unfavourable. Attitudes shape how a civil servant sees the people they serve, and so how they use their discretion.

## 2. The three components

| Component | What it is | Example: an officer's attitude to a slum |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive | Beliefs and knowledge | "Slums grow where cities fail to plan for migrants" |
| Affective | Feelings | Sympathy, or irritation |
| Behavioural | The tendency to act | Prioritising sanitation work there, or avoiding site visits |

This is often called the ABC model. The three parts do not always agree, which is why attitudes can be inconsistent.

## 3. What attitudes do for us

Daniel Katz (1960) described four functions:

1. **Utilitarian**: we like what rewards us.
2. **Knowledge**: attitudes simplify a complex world.
3. **Ego-defensive**: they protect our self-image, as when prejudice hides insecurity.
4. **Value-expressive**: they show what we stand for.

## 4. Do attitudes predict behaviour?

Not reliably. In the 1930s Richard LaPiere travelled across the United States with a Chinese couple and was refused service only once. When the same hotels and restaurants were later asked by letter, most said they would not serve Chinese guests.

Icek Ajzen's theory of planned behaviour explains the gap. Behaviour follows intention, and intention depends on three things: the attitude, the social norm around the person, and how much control they feel they have.

## 5. How attitudes change

- **Cognitive dissonance** (Leon Festinger, 1957): when actions and beliefs clash, people often change the belief to remove the discomfort.
- **Persuasion** works through the source (credible and liked), the message (clear, and two-sided for an informed audience) and the audience. The elaboration likelihood model separates a central route, where people weigh arguments, from a peripheral route, where they follow cues such as a celebrity's endorsement.
- **Contact** with members of a group under equal status and shared goals reduces prejudice.

## 6. Attitudes a civil servant needs

Neutrality towards political parties, empathy for the weaker sections, tolerance of difference, and commitment to public service. Answers can link these to the Code of Conduct and to the foundational values in the syllabus.

## The trap

An attitude is not a value. Values are broad, deep beliefs about what matters, such as justice. Attitudes are evaluations of specific objects, and one value can produce many attitudes.

- Attitude: a learned, fairly lasting evaluation of a person, group, object or idea.
- ABC model: cognitive (beliefs), affective (feelings), behavioural (tendency to act).
- Katz's four functions: utilitarian, knowledge, ego-defensive, value-expressive.
