## 1. Quick anchor

Emotional intelligence is the ability to recognise emotions in oneself and in others, and to use that understanding to guide thought and action. For a civil servant it decides how well technical competence turns into results on the ground.

## 2. Where the idea comes from

- Peter Salovey and John Mayer introduced the term in 1990.
- Daniel Goleman brought it to a wide audience with his 1995 book *Emotional Intelligence*.

## 3. Two models to quote

**Mayer and Salovey's four branches**

1. Perceiving emotions
2. Using emotions to help thinking
3. Understanding emotions
4. Managing emotions

**Goleman's five components**

| Component | In an administrator |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Knows what makes them angry before a tense public hearing |
| Self-regulation | Stays calm when provoked by a delegation |
| Motivation | Keeps working on a slow reform without outside reward |
| Empathy | Understands why evicted families distrust officials |
| Social skills | Builds agreement between departments that pull in different directions |

## 4. Why it matters in the civil services

- **Crisis handling.** A district officer during a flood has to decide fast while people around them panic.
- **Public dealing.** Grievances arrive with anger attached. An officer who hears the feeling as well as the complaint settles more of them.
- **Team leadership.** Subordinates work better for a leader who notices morale.
- **Negotiation.** Land acquisition, strikes and communal tension are settled by people who can read the other side.
- **Personal resilience.** Managing one's own stress prevents burnout and rash decisions.

## 5. How it is built

- Reflection on one's own reactions, for example through a daily journal.
- Listening without preparing a reply.
- Asking for honest feedback from colleagues.
- Field exposure, which builds empathy more reliably than classroom training.

## 6. Limits to mention

Emotional intelligence can be misused to manipulate. It has to be joined to integrity, or it becomes a tool for getting one's way.

## The trap

Do not define it as "being emotional" or "being nice". It is the management of emotion in the service of sound judgement.

- Definition: recognise, understand and manage emotions in oneself and others.
- Salovey and Mayer coined it in 1990. Goleman popularised it in 1995.
- Four branches: perceive, use, understand, manage.
- Goleman's five: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills.
- Uses for an administrator: crisis handling, public dealing, team leadership, negotiation, resilience.
- Built through reflection, listening, feedback and field exposure.
