## 1. The Union Executive and an indirectly elected head of state

Part V (**Articles 52 to 78**) defines the **Union Executive** across five offices: the **President**, **Vice-President**, **Prime Minister**, **Council of Ministers**, and **Attorney General of India**—examiners frequently test whether the Attorney General is included.

Opting for a parliamentary system over a presidential one, the Constituent Assembly split executive authority between a real (*de facto*) head of government (the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, answerable to the Lok Sabha) and a nominal (*de jure*) head of state. **Droupadi Murmu** has served as the 15th President since 25 July 2022, and **C.P. Radhakrishnan** as the 15th Vice-President since 12 September 2025.

> "He is the head of the State but not of the Executive. He represents the Nation but does not rule the Nation. He is the symbol of the nation. His place in the administration is that of a ceremonial device on a seal by which the nation's decisions are made known." — B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 4 November 1948

This explains **indirect election**: a directly elected President would claim a rival popular mandate against the Prime Minister at immense cost for a ceremonial office, yet leaving the choice to Parliament alone would neglect federalism.

## 2. Election, qualifications, term and impeachment

### Electoral College and vote value (Articles 54–55)

Under **Article 54**, the President is elected by an **Electoral College** comprising strictly **elected** representatives: elected MPs of both Houses, elected MLAs of all States, and elected MLAs of **NCT of Delhi and UT of Puducherry** (added by the **70th Amendment Act, 1992**). **Excluded:** nominated MPs and MLAs, **all members** (elected and nominated) of State Legislative Councils (Vidhan Parishads), and members of dissolved Assemblies. Even after the UT of **Jammu and Kashmir** held Assembly elections in 2024, Article 54's Explanation (as of November 2026) still names only Delhi and Puducherry; formally adding J&K requires a constitutional amendment ratified by half the States under Article 368(2), though jurists debate whether Section 13 of the J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 extends Puducherry's Article 239A framework.

**Article 55** secures **uniformity** among States and **parity** between the States and the Union:

- **MLA vote value** = `(State population ÷ Elected MLAs) × (1 ÷ 1,000)` (add 1 if remainder ≥ 500).
- **MP vote value** = `Total value of all MLA votes ÷ Total elected MPs`.

"Population" uses the **1971 Census**—frozen by the 42nd Amendment (1976) and extended by the **84th Amendment Act, 2001** until the first census after 2026—so states controlling population growth are not penalised. Voting uses **proportional representation by single transferable vote (PR-STV)** (quota: `(Total valid votes ÷ 2) + 1`) by **secret ballot**, so party whips and the Tenth Schedule **do not apply**.

### Qualifications, oath, term and disputes (Articles 56–60, 62, 71)

Under **Article 58**, a candidate must be an Indian citizen (**by birth or naturalisation**, unlike the US rule), at least **35 years old**, qualified for the **Lok Sabha**, and hold no **office of profit** (sitting President, VP, Governors, and Union or State Ministers are exempt). Under the **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952** (amended 1997), nomination needs **50 proposers, 50 seconders**, and a **₹15,000** deposit (forfeited below one-sixth of votes).

The oath sits in **Article 60** (not the Third Schedule), administered by the **Chief Justice of India** (or senior-most SC judge), pledging to "preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law". Emoluments cannot be diminished during office (Art 59). The term is **five years** (Art 56), with resignation to the **Vice-President** and **unlimited re-eligibility** (Art 57; only Rajendra Prasad served two terms). Mid-term vacancies are filled within **six months** (Art 62) for a **full five-year term**. Under **Article 71**, election disputes lie **exclusively before the Supreme Court**; acts performed before invalidation stay valid (Art 71(2)), and Electoral College vacancies cannot stall a poll (Art 71(4), 11th Amendment, 1961—confirmed in ***In re: Presidential Poll*, 1974**, when Gujarat's Assembly stood dissolved).

### Impeachment (Article 61)

The President can be removed only by **impeachment** for **"violation of the Constitution"**—a phrase **not defined** in the Constitution. It is a **quasi-judicial** parliamentary process, never yet invoked:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Initiating House (Lok Sabha OR Rajya Sabha)           │
│  Charges signed by >= 1/4th members + 14 days' notice  │
│  Passed by 2/3rd of TOTAL membership of the House      │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Second House investigates (or causes investigation)   │
│  President has right to appear and be represented      │
│  Sustained by 2/3rd of TOTAL membership of the House   │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            ↓
        President stands removed from date of passage
```

> **Prelims pointer:** Watch the classic Electoral College vs Impeachment swap: **nominated MPs** do *not* vote in the election but *do* participate in impeachment; **State and UT MLAs** *do* vote in the election but have *no role* in impeachment. Also, impeachment needs **2/3rd of total membership**—stricter than an Article 368 special majority.

## 3. Powers and functions of the President

Exercised normally on ministerial advice, Union powers run in the President's name across six spheres:

- **Executive (Arts 53, 77):** Executive actions run in the President's name; business rules are framed under **Article 77(3)**. Appoints the PM, Ministers, Attorney General, CAG, CEC/ECs, UPSC, Finance Commission, Governors, SC/HC judges, and NCSC/NCST/NCBC; administers UTs and constitutes Inter-State Councils (Art 263).
- **Legislative:** Integral to **Parliament (Art 79)**; summons and prorogues Houses, dissolves Lok Sabha (Art 85), addresses Parliament (Art 87), and summons **joint sittings (Art 108)**. Nominates **12 Rajya Sabha MPs** (two Anglo-Indian Lok Sabha seats ended in January 2020 via the **104th Amendment, 2019**). Recommends **Article 3** Bills and **Money Bills (Art 117)**; decides non-defection MP disqualifications with binding ECI consultation (Art 103); lays CAG, UPSC and Finance Commission reports.
- **Financial:** Lays the **Annual Financial Statement (Art 112)**; recommends Money Bills and demands for grants (Art 113(3)); controls the **Contingency Fund of India (Art 267)**; constitutes the **Finance Commission** every five years (Art 280).
- **Judicial:** Appoints SC/HC judges; seeks the Supreme Court's non-binding advisory opinion under **Article 143**.
- **Diplomatic, military and emergency:** Concludes treaties in the President's name; serves as **Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces (Art 53(2))** (regulated by Parliament); proclaims Emergencies under **Articles 352, 356 and 360**.

## 4. Three signature powers: veto, ordinances and pardon

### 4.1 Veto power (Articles 111 and 201)

Under **Article 111**, the President may assent to a Bill, **withhold assent**, or **return it** (if not a Money Bill) for reconsideration. India recognises three vetoes—**absolute** (withholding assent, ending the Bill), **suspensive** (returning once; ordinary repassage overrides it), and **pocket** (indefinite inaction, as Article 111 fixes **no time limit**, unlike the US 10-day rule)—but **no qualified veto** (supermajority override).

> **Example:** Presidents Rajendra Prasad (PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954) and R. Venkataraman (MPs' Salary Bill, 1991) used the **absolute veto**. President **Giani Zail Singh** used the **pocket veto** on the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986** over press-freedom concerns.

| Type of Bill | Absolute | Suspensive | Pocket | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary Bill** | Yes | Yes (repassage binds) | Yes | Art 111; repassed Bill must get assent |
| **Money Bill** | Yes | **No** | Yes | Art 111 bars returning Money Bills |
| **Amendment Bill** | **No** | **No** | **No** | **24th Amendment (1971)** mandated assent (Art 368) |
| **Reserved State Bill (Art 201)** | Yes | Yes—**repassage does NOT bind** | Yes | Assembly gets **6 months**; Art 201 omits "shall not withhold assent" |

> **In the news:** **Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 (20 November 2025).** On **8 April 2025**, a two-judge Bench in *State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of Tamil Nadu* fixed assent timelines for Governors (1–3 months, Art 200) and the President (3 months, Art 201) and used Article 142 for "deemed assent" on ten Bills. Answering President Murmu's 14-question reference under **Article 143(1)** (13 May 2025), a five-judge Bench led by CJI B.R. Gavai opined on **20 November 2025** that courts cannot impose rigid timelines or "deemed assent" (terming Art 201 timeline remarks *obiter*), and merits under Articles 200–201 are non-justiciable—yet Governors cannot "withhold assent simpliciter" without returning a Bill, and prolonged unexplained inaction invites a **limited mandamus**.

### 4.2 Ordinance-making power (Article 123)

Descending from Section 42 of the Government of India Act, 1935 (absent in the UK or US), **Article 123** empowers the President to promulgate **ordinances** having the force of an Act of Parliament during recess. Four strict boundaries apply: (1) **both Houses OR either House** must not be in session (valid if one sits, void if both sit); (2) the President (on CoM advice) must be satisfied immediate action is necessary; (3) competence is **co-extensive with Parliament**—subject to Fundamental Rights (an ordinance is "law" under Art 13(3)—***A.K. Roy*, 1982**) and **cannot amend the Constitution**, though it may repeal Acts, operate retrospectively, or be withdrawn anytime; and (4) it **must be laid** before both Houses on reassembly and lapses **six weeks from reassembly** (from the later date if Houses meet separately) unless approved or earlier disapproved. Given Article 85's six-month gap between sessions, maximum unapproved life is **six months and six weeks**.

```text
 Parliament not in session (both Houses OR either House)
                            ↓
  President promulgates Ordinance (on CoM aid & advice)
                            ↓
       Parliament reassembles (mandatory laying)
         ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
         ↓                                     ↓
 Approved by both Houses      Not approved / disapproved
 within 6 weeks               within 6 weeks
         ↓                                     ↓
   Becomes an Act of                 Ordinance lapses
      Parliament              (max life: 6 mo + 6 wks)
```

To curb "ordinance raj", ***R.C. Cooper* (1970)** made presidential satisfaction justiciable for *mala fides* (barred by the 38th Amendment, restored by the **44th Amendment, 1978**). In ***D.C. Wadhwa* (1987)**, the Court struck down Bihar's serial re-promulgation of 256 ordinances. A seven-judge Bench in ***Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar* (2017)** held laying before the legislature is a **mandatory duty**, and rights under a lapsed ordinance do not endure automatically unless irreversible public interest demands it.

> "Re-promulgation of ordinances is a fraud on the Constitution and a sub-version of democratic legislative processes, as laid down in the judgment of the Constitution Bench in D C Wadhwa." — Justice D.Y. Chandrachud, *Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar*, 2 January 2017

### 4.3 Pardoning power (Article 72)

Article 72 empowers the President to grant clemency for (i) **Union-law** offences, (ii) **court-martial** sentences, and (iii) **all death sentences** (including under State laws):

| Form | Legal Effect |
|---|---|
| **Pardon** | Removes **both sentence and conviction**, absolving all penalties and disqualifications |
| **Commutation** | Substitutes a **lighter form** of punishment (e.g., death → rigorous imprisonment) |
| **Remission** | Shortens **duration** without changing character (e.g., 2 years → 1 year rigorous) |
| **Respite** | Awards a **lesser sentence** for a **special fact** (e.g., disability or pregnancy) |
| **Reprieve** | **Temporary stay** of execution (especially death penalty) pending mercy review |

| Feature | President (Article 72) | Governor (Article 161) |
|---|---|---|
| **Offence scope** | Union laws | State laws |
| **Court-martial** | **Yes** | **No power** |
| **Death sentence** | **Can pardon**, suspend, remit or commute | **Cannot pardon**; may suspend, remit or commute under Art 72(3) statutory saving |

Key rulings: ***Maru Ram* (1980)** held clemency runs on binding CoM advice (confirmed for Art 161 in ***A.G. Perarivalan*, 2022**); ***Kehar Singh* (1989)** allowed fresh scrutiny of merits without altering court records and denied any right to oral hearing; ***Epuru Sudhakar* (2006)** enabled limited judicial review for *mala fides*, extraneous considerations or non-application of mind; ***Shatrughan Chauhan* (2014)** ruled unexplained delay violates **Article 21**, warranting death-sentence commutation; and ***Mahendra Vishwanath Kawchale* (Sept 2025)** affirmed that statutory natural-life sentences cannot bar constitutional remission under Articles 72/161.

> **Mains angle:** Mains 2025 asked for a comparison of the pardoning power in India and the USA, its limits, and the meaning of a "pre-emptive pardon". The US President decides alone, only for federal offences and never in cases of impeachment, and may pardon at any time after the act, even before a charge is brought: that is a pre-emptive pardon, as Ford gave Nixon in 1974. India's President acts on ministerial advice (*Maru Ram*), the decision can be reviewed on narrow grounds (*Epuru Sudhakar*), and Article 72 speaks of persons "convicted", so clemency follows conviction and cannot forestall a trial.

## 5. Constitutional position: nominal head or rubber stamp?

**Article 53(1)** vests executive power in the President; **Article 74(1)** provides a Council of Ministers to aid and advise. Courts already treated this advice as binding by convention (***Ram Jawaya Kapur*, 1955**; ***Shamsher Singh*, 1974**). The **42nd Amendment (1976)** made advice expressly binding; the **44th Amendment (1978)** let the President seek **one reconsideration**, after which reconsidered advice binds. **Article 78** obliges the PM to communicate Cabinet decisions and furnish requested information.

Unlike Governors (Article 163), the President has **no constitutional discretion**, only **situational discretion** in three contingencies: appointing a PM in a hung Lok Sabha or sudden death of an incumbent; dismissing a ministry that refuses to quit after losing Lok Sabha confidence; and deciding on dissolution when advised by a ministry without a majority.

> **Mains angle:** Calling the President a "rubber stamp" misses Bagehot's triad—the rights **to be consulted, to encourage, and to warn**. Using Article 78 and the 44th Amendment, **K.R. Narayanan** returned Cabinet advice for President's Rule in UP (1997) and Bihar (1998), and **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** returned the Office of Profit Bill (2006). In coalition eras the President acts as constitutional referee; in majority eras, as non-partisan moral guardian sustained by healthy conventions.

## 6. The Vice-President of India (Articles 63 to 71)

Inspired by the US Vice-President, **Article 63** creates India's second-highest office to preside over the Rajya Sabha and ensure presidential continuity.

Under **Article 66**, the Electoral College comprises **all MPs—elected AND nominated—of both Houses**, excluding State and UT MLAs. Candidates must be Indian citizens, **35 years old**, qualified for the **Rajya Sabha** (contrast President = Lok Sabha), hold no office of profit, and file **20 proposers, 20 seconders** and a **₹15,000** deposit. The **President** administers the oath (**Article 69**); the Supreme Court decides disputes (**Article 71**).

The term is **five years** (Art 67), with unlimited re-election (only S. Radhakrishnan and Hamid Ansari served two terms) and resignation to the President. Mid-term vacancies are filled **"as soon as possible"** (**Article 68(2)**—no six-month rule as in Art 62) for a **full five-year term**. Removal (**Article 67(b)**) needs **no impeachment** and states **no ground**: initiated **only in the Rajya Sabha** after **14 days' notice**, passed by **effective majority** (majority of all the then members) and **agreed to by the Lok Sabha by simple majority**.

> **In the news:** **2025 mid-term Vice-Presidential transition.** Vice-President **Jagdeep Dhankhar** resigned on **21 July 2025** citing health reasons, prompting a mid-term election under Article 68(2). On **9 September 2025**, NDA candidate **C.P. Radhakrishnan** defeated opposition nominee B. Sudershan Reddy (452–300 votes) and took oath on **12 September 2025** as the 15th Vice-President for a full five-year term.

**Dual functions:** As **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Art 64)**, the Vice-President presides like the Speaker (casting only a tie-breaking vote) and draws salary in that role. Under **Article 65**, they **act as President** during a vacancy (maximum **six months**—unlike the US VP who serves the full remainder) or temporary inability, pausing Rajya Sabha Chair duties and drawing the President's salary. If **both** offices are vacant simultaneously, the **President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969** (Art 70) designates the **Chief Justice of India** (or senior-most SC judge)—used once in 1969 when **CJI M. Hidayatullah** acted after President Zakir Husain's death and Acting President V.V. Giri's resignation.

| Feature | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| **Electoral College** | **Elected** MPs + elected MLAs of States, Delhi, Puducherry (Art 54) | **All** MPs (elected + nominated); **no** MLAs (Art 66) |
| **Nomination** | 50 proposers + 50 seconders (₹15,000) | 20 proposers + 20 seconders (₹15,000) |
| **Eligibility / Oath** | **Lok Sabha** (Art 58) / Oath by CJI (Art 60) | **Rajya Sabha** (Art 66) / Oath by President (Art 69) |
| **Removal** | **Either House**, 1/4th sign, 14-day notice; **2/3rd of total membership** in both Houses; ground: violation of Constitution (Art 61) | **Only RS**, 14-day notice; **effective majority** (RS) + **simple majority** (LS); no stated ground (Art 67(b)) |
| **Mid-term vacancy** | Within **6 months** (Art 62) | **"As soon as possible"** (Art 68(2)) |
| **Succession** | VP → CJI → senior-most SC judge (1969 Act) | None; Deputy Chairman presides in RS |

<!-- distilled -->

- **Union Executive (Arts 52–78):** President, Vice-President, PM, Council of Ministers, and **Attorney General**.
- **Incumbents (Nov 2026):** **Droupadi Murmu** (15th President, July 2022); **C.P. Radhakrishnan** (15th VP, 12 Sept 2025, after Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned 21 July 2025).
- **Presidential Electoral College (Art 54):** **Elected** MPs + elected MLAs of States, **Delhi, Puducherry** (70th Amendment, 1992); excludes nominated members, all MLCs, dissolved Assemblies; **J&K UT** not named.
- **Vote formula (Art 55):** Inter-state uniformity + Union–State parity; **1971 Census** frozen till after 2026 (**84th Amendment**); PR-STV + **secret ballot** (no whip).
- **Eligibility & disputes (Arts 56–62, 71):** Citizen (birth/naturalisation), 35 yrs, **Lok Sabha**, 50+50 proposers/seconders, ₹15,000, oath by CJI (Art 60), unlimited re-election, vacancy filled in **6 months** (full 5-yr term); SC sole arbiter (*In re: Presidential Poll*, 1974).
- **Impeachment (Art 61):** Ground: **"violation of the Constitution"** (undefined); **either House** (1/4th sign, 14-day notice); **2/3rd of total membership** in both Houses. Nominated MPs participate (not in election); MLAs do not (vote in election).
- **Nominations:** 12 Rajya Sabha MPs; 2 Anglo-Indian Lok Sabha seats ended Jan 2020 (**104th Amendment, 2019**).
- **Vetoes (Arts 111, 201):** Absolute, Suspensive (barred on Money & Amendment Bills), Pocket (no limit—Zail Singh, 1986); no Qualified veto. **24th Amendment** mandated assent on Amendment Bills; under **Art 201**, repassed State Bills do not bind President.
- **Special Reference No. 1 of 2025 (20 Nov 2025):** 5-judge Bench rejected rigid timelines and "deemed assent" (*TN Governor*, Apr 2025), barred indefinite "withholding simpliciter", and allowed limited mandamus.
- **Ordinances (Art 123):** Issued when **both OR either House** is in recess; **cannot amend Constitution** (*A.K. Roy*, 1982); lapses **6 weeks** after reassembly (max **6 months + 6 weeks**).
- **Ordinance guardrails:** *Mala fides* justiciable (*R.C. Cooper*, 1970; **44th Amendment**); serial re-promulgation void (*D.C. Wadhwa*, 1987); ***Krishna Kumar Singh* (2017)** ruled laying mandatory and re-promulgation a **"fraud on the Constitution"**.
- **Pardon (Art 72):** Pardon (erases guilt + sentence), Commutation (lighter form), Remission (shorter period), Respite (special fact), Reprieve (stay). Covers Union laws, **court-martial**, and **all death sentences**.
- **Art 72 vs Art 161:** Governor **cannot pardon** death sentences (may suspend/remit/commute under Art 72(3)) and has **no court-martial power**.
- **Clemency cases:** CoM binds (*Maru Ram*, 1980; *Perarivalan*, 2022); no oral hearing (*Kehar Singh*, 1989); limited review (*Epuru Sudhakar*, 2006); delay violates Art 21 (*Shatrughan Chauhan*, 2014); natural-life sentences do not bar remission (*Kawchale*, 2025).
- **Position (Arts 74, 78):** 42nd Amendment made advice binding; **44th Amendment** added one reconsideration. Only **situational discretion** (hung LS, defeated CoM).
- **VP election & removal (Arts 66–68):** **All MPs** (elected + nominated), **no MLAs**; 20+20 proposers/seconders; **Rajya Sabha** eligibility; oath by President (Art 69). Removal: no impeachment or ground; **only RS** (14-day notice), **effective majority** (RS) + **simple majority** (LS); vacancy filled **"as soon as possible"** (full 5-yr term).
- **Acting President:** VP acts up to **6 months** (Art 65), pauses RS duties, draws President's salary. Simultaneous vacancy → CJI / senior-most SC judge (**1969 Act**, Art 70; **CJI M. Hidayatullah**, 1969).
