## 1. Public Bills, Private Member's Bills and pre-legislative consultation

Every law starts in Parliament as a **Bill** and becomes an **Act** only after passing both Houses and receiving Presidential assent (**Articles 107 to 111**). Bills are classified by *mover* into **Public (Government) Bills** and **Private Member's Bills** (any non-minister MP—including ruling-party MPs—is a "private member"), and by *content* into **Ordinary Bills**, **Money Bills**, **Financial Bills (I & II)** and **Constitution Amendment Bills**.

| Feature | Public (Government) Bill | Private Member's Bill |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduced by** | A Union Minister | Any MP other than a Minister |
| **Advance notice** | **7 days** | **1 month** |
| **Drafting** | Ministry with **Law Ministry** | Responsibility of the individual MP |
| **Schedule** | Any working day | **Fridays** (last 2.5 hrs, alternate Fridays) |
| **Defeat impact** | May signal loss of confidence / resignation | **No impact** on government survival |

> **Example:** Only **14 Private Member's Bills** have ever become Acts—and **none since 1970**, when the **Supreme Court (Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction) Bill, 1968** (piloted by independent MP **Anand Narain Mulla**) received assent on **9 August 1970** (earlier landmarks included Feroze Gandhi's *Parliamentary Proceedings Act, 1956*). Tiruchi Siva's *Rights of Transgender Persons Bill* passed the Rajya Sabha unanimously in 2015, but stalled in the Lok Sabha.

**Pre-Legislative Consultation Policy (PLCP), 2014:** Adopted in **February 2014** (drawing on NCRWC and NAC proposals), the PLCP instructs ministries to publish draft Bills and subordinate rules for **at least 30 days** before Cabinet approval, alongside a simple explanatory note, **financial, environmental and fundamental-rights impact assessments**, and a published feedback summary. Because it is a **non-binding executive guideline**, compliance has been weak: **PRS Legislative Research** found **227 of 301 Bills (~75%)** introduced after 2014 bypassed prior consultation, over half of the remainder missed the 30-day window, and the Law Ministry tracks no central compliance register.

## 2. Ordinary Bills: the five-stage journey (Articles 107, 111)

Any Bill outside financial subjects and Article 368 amendments is an **Ordinary Bill**. Introduced in **either House** by a minister or private member without prior Presidential recommendation, it traverses **five stages**:

1. **First Reading:** Mover seeks leave of the House, reads the title and objectives (normally no debate unless legislative competence is challenged), and the Bill is published in the **Gazette of India**.
2. **Second Reading** (the pivotal stage—three sub-stages):
   - *General Discussion:* Principles debated; the House may take it up immediately, refer it to a **Select Committee** (same House) or **Joint Committee** (both Houses, with the other House's concurrence), or circulate it for public opinion. Separately, the Chair may refer it to a **Departmentally Related Standing Committee (DRSC)**.
   - *Committee Stage:* Clause-by-clause scrutiny with expert hearings, without altering the Bill's core principle.
   - *Consideration Stage:* Clause-by-clause floor discussion, amendments and voting.
3. **Third Reading:** Debate limited to overall acceptance or rejection; **no substantive amendments** (only formal, verbal or consequential corrections). A **simple majority of members present and voting** passes it.
4. **Second House:** Repeats all three readings with **four options**: (a) pass as-is, (b) pass with amendments and return, (c) reject, or (d) take no action—if **>6 months** elapse without passage, a **deadlock** permits a **Joint Sitting (Article 108)**.
5. **Presidential Assent (Article 111):** The President may **assent** (becomes an Act), **withhold assent** (absolute veto), or **return once** for reconsideration (suspensive veto—repassage with or without amendments makes assent mandatory). No timeline permits a **pocket veto**.

```text
  STAGE 1: First Reading (Either House + Gazette)
                         ↓
  STAGE 2: Second Reading (General -> Committee -> Clauses)
                         ↓
  STAGE 3: Third Reading (Overall pass/reject; simple maj.)
                         ↓
  STAGE 4: Second House (Repeats 3 readings)
    ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
    ↓                                         ↓
  Passes                           Rejects / Disagrees /
  as-is                            Delays > 6 months
    │                                         ↓
    │                              Joint Sitting (Art 108)
    └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                         ↓
  STAGE 5: President (Art 111: Assent / Withhold / Return)
```

## 3. Money Bills and the constitutional controversy (Articles 109, 110)

### Exact constitutional definition (Article 110)

Under **Article 110(1)**, a Bill is a **Money Bill** if it contains **"ONLY"** provisions dealing with all or any of **seven** matters:
- **(a)** imposition, abolition, remission, alteration or regulation of any **tax**;
- **(b)** regulation of **borrowing of money** or guarantees by the Government of India;
- **(c)** custody of, or payments into/withdrawals from, the **Consolidated Fund of India (CFI)** or **Contingency Fund of India**;
- **(d)** **appropriation** of money out of the CFI;
- **(e)** declaring any expenditure **charged on the CFI** or increasing it;
- **(f)** receipt/custody/issue of money on account of the CFI or **Public Account of India**, or **audit of Union or State accounts**;
- **(g)** any matter **incidental** to (a)–(f).

**What is NOT a Money Bill (Article 110(2)):** A Bill is **NOT** a Money Bill merely because it provides for **fines or pecuniary penalties**, **fees for licences or services rendered**, or **taxes imposed by local authorities for local purposes**.

> **Prelims pointer:** Nail the word **"ONLY"** in Art 110(1) and the three Art 110(2) exclusions (**fines, licence/service fees, local-body taxes**)! Also remember: **audit of Union OR State accounts** *is* a Money Bill subject, and every Money Bill is a **Government Bill**.

### Special procedure (Articles 109, 110(3)–(4), 117(1))

A Money Bill is introduced **ONLY in the Lok Sabha**, **ONLY with the President's prior recommendation**, and **only by a minister**. Under **Article 110(3)–(4)**, the **Speaker's decision is "final"** and their signed certificate is endorsed on transmission to the Rajya Sabha and the President. The **Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend** it—only make **non-binding recommendations** within **14 days** (deemed passed as cleared by LS if not returned in 14 days). There is **NO Joint Sitting**, and under Article 111 the President may assent or withhold, **never return** a Money Bill.

### The Money Bill controversy and judicial review

Classifying wider regulatory statutes as Money Bills to bypass a non-majority Rajya Sabha became a defining constitutional flashpoint. In ***K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar-5)* (26 September 2018)**, a five-judge Bench upheld the **Aadhaar Act, 2016** as a Money Bill by **4:1** (linking Section 7 subsidies to the CFI), while **Justice D.Y. Chandrachud** dissented sharply, holding the Speaker's certificate reviewable for constitutional illegality and terming misuse a "fraud on the Constitution".

> "Passing of a Bill as a Money Bill, when it does not qualify for it, damages the delicate balance of bicameralism which is a part of the basic structure of the Constitution." — Justice D.Y. Chandrachud (dissenting), *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*, 26 September 2018

When the Union subsequently passed **Part XIV of the Finance Act, 2017** (restructuring tribunals) as a Money Bill—alongside PMLA and electoral-bond amendments—a co-equal five-judge Bench in ***Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank* (13 November 2019)** doubted *Puttaswamy*'s cursory analysis of *"only"* in Article 110(1) and referred Money Bill certification and judicial review to a **seven-judge Constitution Bench**.

> **In the news:** **Seven-judge reference still pending (Nov 2026).** Nearly seven years after ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)** (last listed October 2023), the seven-judge Bench has yet to decide the constitutional scope of Article 110(1), leaving executive bypass of the Rajya Sabha judicially unresolved.

## 4. Financial Bills (I & II) and Constitution Amendment Bills

> **Remember:** **All Money Bills are Financial Bills, but NOT all Financial Bills are Money Bills!** Fiscal legislation outside strictly "only Art 110" Bills falls under **Financial Bills Category I (Art 117(1))** and **Category II (Art 117(3))**.

**Financial Bill (Category I) — Article 117(1):** Contains any **Article 110 matter PLUS general legislation**. It matches a Money Bill in **ONLY TWO respects**: (1) introduced **ONLY in the Lok Sabha**, and (2) needs the **President's prior recommendation** (proviso: *no* recommendation needed for amendments **reducing or abolishing a tax**). In **all other respects** it follows the **Ordinary Bill** route—**Rajya Sabha can amend or reject** it, a **Joint Sitting** is allowed, and the President can **return it**.

**Financial Bill (Category II) — Article 117(3):** Involves **expenditure from the CFI** but contains **NO Article 110 matters**. It follows the **Ordinary Bill** route at introduction—**either House**, **NO prior Presidential recommendation**—with **ONE critical twist**: **neither House can PASS it unless the President recommends its consideration!** Rajya Sabha is co-equal, Joint Sitting is allowed, and the President can return it.

> **Prelims pointer:** **Financial Bill I** = Presidential recommendation at **introduction** (LS only). **Financial Bill II** = NO recommendation at introduction (either House), **mandatory Presidential recommendation at the consideration stage**!

**Constitution Amendment Bills (Article 368 — procedural profile):** Detailed amendment doctrine sits in *Constitutional Amendment*; procedurally, an Article 368 Bill may start in **either House**, by a **minister or private member**, with **NO prior Presidential recommendation**. It requires a **special majority** (>50% of total membership + >=2/3rd present and voting) in **each House separately**—**NO Joint Sitting**—plus ratification by **at least half of State Assemblies** (simple majority) for federal changes, and **mandatory Presidential assent** (**24th Amendment, 1971**).

> **Example:** On **17 April 2026**, the **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026** (850 LS seats; 2011 delimitation) failed in Lok Sabha on a recorded division (**298–230** out of 528, short of the 352 two-thirds mark)—a live display of Article 368's supermajority hurdle.

| Feature | Ordinary Bill | Money Bill (Art 110) | Financial Bill I (Art 117(1)) | Financial Bill II (Art 117(3)) | Const. Amendment (Art 368) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intro House** | **Either House** | **Lok Sabha ONLY** | **Lok Sabha ONLY** | **Either House** | **Either House** |
| **Mover** | Minister / Private MP | **Minister ONLY** | Minister (needs Pres. rec.) | Minister / Private MP | Minister / Private MP |
| **Pres. rec.** | **No** | **Yes** (intro) | **Yes** (intro) | **No at intro**; **Yes at consideration** | **No** |
| **Speaker cert.** | No | **Yes (final)** | No | No | No |
| **RS power** | **Co-equal** | **Recommend only (14 days)** | **Co-equal** | **Co-equal** | **Co-equal** (separate passage) |
| **Majority** | Simple | Simple | Simple | Simple | **Special** (+ half States if federal) |
| **Joint Sitting** | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
| **Art 111 return** | **Yes** (once) | **No** | **Yes** (once) | **Yes** (once) | **No** (assent **mandatory**) |

## 5. Joint Sitting of both Houses (Article 108)

Summoned by the **President** after notifying both Houses, a **Joint Sitting** resolves deadlocks when, after passage by one House and transmission to the other: (1) the other House **rejects** the Bill; (2) the Houses **finally disagree** on amendments; or (3) **>6 months** elapse from receipt by the other House without passage (excluding prorogation or adjournments >**4 consecutive days**).

- **Scope:** **ONLY Ordinary Bills and Financial Bills (I & II)**—**NEVER Money Bills or Art 368 Bills**.
- **Presiding hierarchy (Article 118(4)):** **(1) Speaker (LS)** → **(2) Deputy Speaker (LS)** → **(3) Deputy Chairman (RS)** → **(4) member chosen by sitting**. **The Vice-President (RS Chairman) NEVER presides** because they are **not a member of either House**!
- **Quorum, rules and voting:** Quorum is **1/10th of combined total strength**; governed by **Lok Sabha Rules**; decided by **simple majority of combined members present and voting** (favouring LS's 543 vs RS's 245). Under **Article 108(4)**, no new amendments are allowed except those caused by **delay** (on rejection/6-month lapse) or tied to **disagreement/delay** (when amended by the second House).

| Sitting | Date | Bill | Trigger | Presided by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **6 & 9 May 1961** | **Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1959** | Amendment disagreement | Speaker **M.A. Ayyangar** |
| **2nd** | **16 May 1978** | **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill, 1977** | Rejected by RS | Speaker **K.S. Hegde** |
| **3rd** | **26 March 2002** | **Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002** | Rejected by RS | **Deputy Speaker P.M. Sayeed** (Speaker Balayogi died earlier in Mar 2002) |

> **Prelims pointer:** Never forget: (1) **VP / RS Chairman NEVER presides** over a Joint Sitting, while **Deputy Chairman of RS** is third in line; (2) Women's Reservation was *never* passed via Joint Sitting; (3) **State Legislatures have NO Joint Sitting** (Art 197 lets the Assembly override the Council after 3 + 1 = 4 months).

## 6. Lapsing of Bills on Lok Sabha dissolution (Articles 107, 108(5))

Remember first: **prorogation NEVER lapses any pending Bill** (**Article 107(3)**)—only notices lapse. On **dissolution of the Lok Sabha**, six airtight rules govern (**Articles 107(4)–(5), 108(5)**):

| Bill status at Lok Sabha dissolution | Fate | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Pending in Lok Sabha** (originated in LS or sent from RS) | **LAPSES** | Art 107(5) |
| **2. Passed by Lok Sabha, pending in Rajya Sabha** | **LAPSES** | Art 107(5) |
| **3. Pending in Rajya Sabha, NOT passed by Lok Sabha** | **SURVIVES** | Art 107(4) — LS never touched it |
| **4. Disagreement where President notified Joint Sitting BEFORE dissolution** | **SURVIVES** | **Art 108(5)** |
| **5. Passed by both Houses, awaiting Presidential assent** | **SURVIVES** | Parliamentary stage complete |
| **6. Passed by both Houses, returned by President for reconsideration** | **SURVIVES** | Art 111 — new LS reconsiders |

```text
  DID THE DISSOLVING LOK SABHA TOUCH THE BILL?
  ├── NO (pending solely in RS) ────────────► SURVIVES
  └── YES
       ├── Already passed BOTH Houses?
       │    (awaiting assent / returned) ───► SURVIVES
       ├── Joint Sitting notified prior
       │    to dissolution (Art 108(5))? ───► SURVIVES
       └── Still pending in LS OR RS? ──────► LAPSES
```

> **Remember:** A Bill before a **Lok Sabha Select Committee** lapses on dissolution, whereas a Bill before a **Rajya Sabha Select Committee** (not passed by LS) and assurances before the Committee on Government Assurances survive.

## 7. Delegated legislation and improving law-making quality: Mains analysis

**Delegated (subordinate) legislation:** Because Parliament cannot legislate every technical detail, parent Acts delegate **rules, regulations and bye-laws** to the Executive for flexibility and expertise. In ***In Re: Delhi Laws Act* (1951)**, a seven-judge Bench set the constitutional boundary: Parliament **cannot delegate its "essential legislative function"** (core policy and standards). Safeguards include **laying rules before Parliament** (typically 30 session days for modification/annulment), oversight by each House's **Committee on Subordinate Legislation** (15 MPs; checks overreach and urges rule-framing within **six months**), and **judicial review** for *ultra vires*.

**The scrutiny deficit and way forward:** **PRS Legislative Research** shows Bills referred to committees plunging from **60% (14th LS)** and **71% (15th LS)** to **25% (16th LS)** and **16% (17th LS)** (where **58%** cleared within two weeks and **35%** had under an hour of LS debate). In the **18th LS (up to Monsoon 2026)**, marquee Bills like *One Nation, One Election* and the *130th Amendment Bill* went to Joint Committees, yet routine **DRSC** referrals stayed rare (11 of 42 Bills sent to committees by Winter 2025; zero newly sent to Standing Committees in Monsoon 2026). Elevating legislative craft demands five steps: (1) **statutory backing for the 2014 PLCP** (30-day multi-lingual drafts + published comments); (2) **default DRSC referral** unless waived by reasoned House vote; (3) mandatory **Legislative Impact Assessments** (fiscal, federal, environmental, rights—echoing **NCRWC, 2002**) and post-legislative audits; (4) early decision on the ***Rojer Mathew* seven-judge Money Bill reference**; and (5) dedicated **non-partisan research staff** for MPs.

> **Mains angle:** Anchor GS-2 answers in **PRS data** (**71% → 16%** committee drop; **75% PLCP bypass**; sparse 18th LS DRSC referrals), link ***In Re Delhi Laws* (1951)** with ***Rojer Mathew* (2019)**, and conclude with **statutory PLCP + default DRSC referral + Legislative Impact Assessments**.

- **Public vs Private Member's Bills:** Public = **7 days' notice** (Ministers); Private = **1 month's notice** (non-minister MPs, Fridays). Only **14 Private Member's Bills** ever enacted—last in **1970** (*SC Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction Bill*, Anand Narain Mulla).
- **PLCP (Feb 2014):** Non-binding **30-day** public consultation + impact assessments before Cabinet approval; PRS found **227 of 301 Bills (~75%)** bypassed it.
- **Ordinary Bills (Arts 107, 111):** Five stages—First Reading (intro + Gazette), Second Reading (General → Committee → Clause-by-clause), Third Reading (overall vote, **no substantive amendments**), Second House (4 options; >6 months = deadlock), President's Assent (assent / withhold / return once).
- **Money Bill definition (Art 110(1)):** Contains **"ONLY"** provisions on taxes, Union borrowing/guarantees, CFI/Contingency Fund custody/payments, appropriation, charged expenditure, CFI/Public Account receipts or **Union/State audit**, and incidental matters.
- **NOT a Money Bill (Art 110(2)):** **Fines/pecuniary penalties**, **licence/service fees**, and **local-body taxes**.
- **Money Bill procedure (Arts 109–110):** **LS ONLY**, **President's prior recommendation**, **Minister ONLY**, Speaker's certificate **"final"**; **RS cannot amend/reject** (recommend within **14 days**, else deemed passed); **NO Joint Sitting**; President **cannot return**.
- **Money Bill judicial review:** ***Puttaswamy (Aadhaar-5)* (2018)** upheld Aadhaar 4:1; **Justice Chandrachud** called misuse a **"fraud on the Constitution"**. ***Rojer Mathew* (Nov 2019)** (Finance Act 2017 tribunals) referred Art 110 / Speaker finality to a **7-judge Bench**—still **pending Nov 2026**.
- **Fiscal maxim:** **All Money Bills are Financial Bills, NOT all Financial Bills are Money Bills.**
- **Financial Bill I (Art 117(1)):** Art 110 + general legislation. Like Money Bill in **ONLY 2 ways**: **LS only** + **President's prior recommendation** (no rec. to reduce/abolish tax). Otherwise Ordinary Bill (RS co-equal, Joint Sitting, President can return).
- **Financial Bill II (Art 117(3)):** CFI expenditure, **no** Art 110 matters. **Either House, NO prior recommendation** at intro, BUT **President's recommendation mandatory at consideration stage**! RS co-equal, Joint Sitting, President can return.
- **Constitution Amendment (Art 368):** Either House, Minister/Private MP, **no prior Pres. rec.**, **special majority** in each House **separately**, **NO Joint Sitting**, half-State ratification if federal, **mandatory Presidential assent** (**24th Amdt, 1971**). Example: **131st Amdt Bill** defeated **17 April 2026 (298–230)**.
- **Joint Sitting triggers (Art 108):** Summoned by **President** on rejection, final amendment disagreement, or **>6 months' lapse** (excluding prorogation / >4-day adjournment).
- **Joint Sitting rules:** **ONLY Ordinary & Financial Bills (I & II)**—**NEVER Money or Art 368 Bills**. Quorum **1/10th combined**; **LS Rules**; **simple majority** of combined present & voting.
- **Joint Sitting Chair (Art 118(4)):** **Speaker → Deputy Speaker → Deputy Chairman (RS) → chosen member**. **VP (RS Chairman) NEVER presides** (not an MP!).
- **3 historic Joint Sittings:** **1961** Dowry Prohibition (Ayyangar); **1978** Banking Service Commission Repeal (Hegde); **2002** POTA (**Deputy Speaker P.M. Sayeed**). **States have NO Joint Sitting** (Art 197: 3 + 1 = 4 months).
- **Dissolution lapsing (Arts 107, 108(5)):** **Prorogation NEVER lapses Bills**. On LS dissolution, Bills **pending in LS** or **passed by LS & pending in RS** **LAPSE**.
- **Surviving LS dissolution:** (1) Pending in **RS, NOT passed by LS**; (2) **Joint Sitting notified BEFORE dissolution** (Art 108(5)); (3) Passed by both Houses, **awaiting assent**; (4) **Returned by President** (Art 111).
- **Delegated legislation:** ***In Re Delhi Laws* (1951)** barred delegating **"essential legislative function"**; policed via parliamentary laying, **Committee on Subordinate Legislation** (15 MPs; 6-month rule norm), and judicial review.
- **Scrutiny deficit (PRS):** Committee referrals dropped **60% (14th) / 71% (15th) → 25% (16th) → 16% (17th LS)**; sparse DRSC referrals in **18th LS (2024–2026)**.
- **Mains reforms:** Statutory PLCP, default DRSC referral, mandatory **Legislative Impact Assessments** (NCRWC 2002), ***Rojer Mathew* 7-judge ruling**, and MP research support.
