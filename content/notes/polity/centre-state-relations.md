## 1. Constitutional architecture of centre–state relations

The Constitution divides Union and State powers across **three axes**: **legislative** (Part XI, Articles 245–255), **administrative** (Part XI, Articles 256–263), and **financial** (Part XII, Articles 268–293), with **no judicial division**—India operates a single, **integrated judiciary** enforcing both central and state laws. Modelled on the **Government of India Act, 1935** and Canada's Constitution, Parts XI and XII build a centripetal tilt to protect unity and a single economic market while preserving state autonomy in normal times. (Conceptual federalism, the Finance Commission and GST Council, emergencies, and inter-state councils are covered in companion notes.)

## 2. Legislative relations: territory, the Seventh Schedule and doctrines

### Territorial extent of laws (Article 245)

Under **Article 245(1)**, Parliament legislates for all or part of India, while a State Legislature legislates only for its state. Only **Parliament** can enact **extra-territorial laws** (Article 245(2)); state laws apply outside state borders only with a genuine **territorial nexus** (***State of Bombay v. R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala***, 1957).

Parliament's territorial reach carries three regional exceptions: under **Article 240**, the President issues regulations (able to repeal or amend parliamentary Acts) for **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, and Ladakh**, and for **Puducherry** *only* when its Assembly is suspended or dissolved; under the **Fifth Schedule**, the **Governor** may bar or modify parliamentary or state Acts in Scheduled Areas; and under the **Sixth Schedule**, the **Governor** modifies parliamentary Acts in **Assam**, whereas the **President** does so for **Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram** (the Governor handling state Acts).

> **Prelims pointer:** Under the **Sixth Schedule**, the **Governor** modifies parliamentary Acts for **Assam**, while the **President** does so for **Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram**.

### The Seventh Schedule (Article 246), Article 246A and residuary powers

**Article 246** divides subjects into three Lists in the **Seventh Schedule** (from the 1935 Act; Concurrent List from Australia).

| Seventh Schedule List | Original | Pre-GST | Active (post-101st Amdt) | Competence & examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **List I — Union** | 97 | 100 | **98** | **Parliament:** defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency, atomic energy, railways, citizenship |
| **List II — State** | 66 | 61 | **59** | **States (normal times):** public order, police, health, agriculture, land, local government, liquor |
| **List III — Concurrent** | 47 | 52 | **52** | **Both tiers:** criminal law, marriage, contracts, trade unions, electricity, education, forests |

Lettered insertions (`2A`, `92A`, `92B`) lifted pre-2016 counts to **100, 61, and 52** while final numbers stayed 97 and 47. The **101st Amendment Act, 2016** (GST) omitted **List I Entries 92 & 92C** and **List II Entries 52 & 55** (substituting List I Entry 84 and List II Entries 54 & 62), leaving strict active totals of **98, 59, and 52**.

Two amendments reshaped this design. The **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** moved **five subjects** from List II to List III: **education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, and administration of justice (constitution and organisation of all courts except the Supreme Court and High Courts)**. The **101st Amendment Act, 2016** inserted **Article 246A**, giving Parliament and States **simultaneous concurrent power** over GST, while keeping **inter-state supply and imports** exclusively with Parliament. **Residuary powers (Article 248 + Entry 97, List I)**—including residuary taxes—vest solely in **Parliament**, following **Canada** (unlike the USA/Australia or the 1935 Act's Governor-General).

> **Remember:** Five subjects shifted by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** (**E-F-W-W-A**): **E**ducation, **F**orests, **W**eights & measures, **W**ild animals & birds, **A**dministration of justice (lower courts).

### Interpretive doctrines of legislative competence

Courts resolve list overlaps through five doctrines:

- **Pith and Substance:** Examines a law's *true character*; incidental encroachment on another list stands if the core falls within competence (***State of Bombay v. F.N. Balsara***, 1951).
- **Colourable Legislation:** *"What cannot be done directly cannot be done indirectly"*—striking down disguised encroachments (***K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo v. State of Orissa***, 1953); tests **competence**, not motive.
- **Harmonious Construction:** Reconciles overlapping entries so none becomes redundant; irreconcilable conflict follows **Article 246** supremacy (**Union > Concurrent > State**).
- **Territorial Nexus:** Permits extra-territorial state reach only if the nexus is **real, not illusory**, and linked to the liability.
- **Repugnancy (Article 254):** Parliamentary law prevails on Concurrent conflicts (Art. 254(1)), except under **Article 254(2)** where a state law with **Presidential assent** prevails *within that state*—subject to the **proviso** letting Parliament later amend, vary, or **repeal** it.

> **In the news:** Two **nine-judge Bench** verdicts in 2024 reshaped Seventh Schedule federalism. In ***MADA v. SAIL*** (**25 July 2024**, 8:1, Justice B.V. Nagarathna dissenting), the Court overruled *India Cement* (1989), holding mining **royalty is not a tax**, States may tax mineral rights (**Entry 50, List II**) and mineral-bearing land (**Entry 49, List II**), and the MMDR Act (**Entry 54, List I**) does not bar that power; a **14 August 2024** order allowed recovering arrears since **1 April 2005** over 12 years from **1 April 2026**. Entry 50 itself makes the States' power "subject to any limitations imposed by Parliament by law relating to mineral development", and Parliament has now used that opening: the **MMDR Amendment Act, 2026** (assent in August 2026) adds **Section 9D**, which bars States from taxing mineral rights or mineral-bearing land except on conditions the Centre prescribes, and treats levies not yet collected as invalid. Whether Parliament can restrict Entry 49 (taxes on land) in this way is the question the amendment leaves open. In ***State of UP v. Lalta Prasad Vaish*** (**23 October 2024**, 8:1), a nine-judge Bench overruled *Synthetics and Chemicals* (1990), placing **industrial alcohol** within **"intoxicating liquor"** (**Entry 8, List II**) against Union Entry 52, List I claims.

## 3. Parliamentary legislation in the state field and control over state bills

In **five extraordinary situations**, Parliament may legislate directly on State List subjects:

| Article | Trigger & majority | Expiry of law | State Legislature's power |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Art. 249** | RS resolution in **"national interest"** (**2/3 present & voting**) | Valid **1 yr** (renewable); ends **6 mo** after expiry | Keeps concurrent power; Central law prevails |
| **Art. 250** | **National Emergency** (Art. 352) | Ends **6 mo** after Emergency ceases | Keeps concurrent power; Central law prevails |
| **Art. 252** | Resolutions by **2+ States** (applies to requesting/adopting states) | Permanent until Parliament alters it | **Surrendered:** **only Parliament** amends/repeals (e.g., Wildlife Act 1972, Water Act 1974) |
| **Art. 253** | Implementing **international treaties/conventions** (no state consent) | Permanent | Central law overrides State law |
| **Art. 356** | **President's Rule** in a State | **Survives** after rule ends | Restored Assembly may later repeal/alter it |

> **Prelims pointer:** **Art. 249** requires RS **2/3 present and voting** (no absolute-majority clause). Under **Art. 252**, requesting states **cannot** amend or repeal the Act. **Art. 356** laws do *not* expire after six months.

### Centre's control over state legislation

The Union checks state bills through three provisions. Under **Articles 200–201**, a Governor may reserve a bill for the President (mandatory if it endangers the High Court's position, or for Arts. 31A, 31C, 254(2) immunity); under Article 201, the President is **not bound** to assent even to a re-passed bill (an effective **absolute veto**). Under **Article 304(b)**, state bills restricting trade or commerce need **prior Presidential sanction** before introduction (cured by later assent under **Article 255**). Under **Article 360** (Financial Emergency), the President may require reservation of State **Money Bills** and financial bills.

> **In the news:** In ***State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor*** (**8 April 2025**, `2025 INSC 481`), a two-judge Bench fixed 1–3 month timelines under Arts. 200–201 and invoked Art. 142 to declare ten withheld bills "deemed assented". Answering a 14-question **Article 143(1)** Reference, a unanimous **five-judge Bench** in ***Special Reference No. 1 of 2025*** (**20 November 2025**, `2025 INSC 1333`) held courts **cannot impose rigid timelines** or **"deem assent"**, though prolonged, unexplained, indefinite inaction remains open to **limited judicial scrutiny**.

## 4. Administrative relations (Part XI, Articles 256–263)

Part XI, Chapter II (**Articles 256–263**) synchronises Union and State administration through co-extensive authority, central directions, mutual delegation, and shared institutions.

### Executive power, central directions and Article 365

Executive power is **co-extensive with legislative power**: the Union administers List I and treaties (**Article 73**), States administer List II (**Article 162**), and on **Concurrent List** subjects executive power **ordinarily stays with the States** unless the Constitution or Parliament vests it in the Centre.

**Article 256** obliges states to ensure **compliance with parliamentary laws** and follow Union directions; **Article 257** bars states from **impeding** Union executive power and allows Union directions on **national/military communications** and **railway protection** (Union reimburses extra costs, arbitrated by a CJI nominee if disputed). Elsewhere, the Centre may direct states on **ST welfare** (**Article 339(2)**) and **mother-tongue primary instruction** (**Article 350A**). Under **Article 365** (Part XVIII), disobeying any constitutional Union direction lets the President hold that state governance **cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution**, triggering **Article 356**.

### Mutual delegation (Articles 258 and 258A) and integrative mechanisms

Delegation is asymmetric: under **Article 258(1)**, the **President** (*with State consent*) may entrust Union functions to a State; under **Article 258(2)**, **Parliament** by law on a Union subject may confer powers/duties on state officers **even without State consent** (costs paid under Art. 258(3)). Conversely, **Article 258A** (**7th Amendment, 1956**) lets a **Governor** (*with Union consent*) entrust state functions to the Union; States lack unilateral delegation power.

Six constitutional mechanisms sustain daily cooperation:

- **Full Faith and Credit (Article 261):** Nationwide recognition of public acts, records, and judicial proceedings; final **civil** judgments (not criminal sentences) execute anywhere in India.
- **Inter-State Bodies (Articles 262–263):** Adjudication of inter-state river disputes (Art. 262) and the Inter-State Council (Art. 263).
- **All-India Services (Article 312):** **IAS, IPS, and IFoS (1966)** are centrally recruited, under **immediate State control** yet **ultimate Union disciplinary control** (dismissal). Creating a new AIS requires an RS resolution (**2/3 present and voting**).
- **Public Service Commissions (Articles 315–317):** State PSC members are appointed by the Governor but **removed only by the President** (after SC inquiry); **Joint PSC** members (created by Parliament on state request) are appointed and removed by the President.
- **Integrated Judiciary, ECI and CAG:** President appoints HC judges (common HCs under Art. 231); ECI and CAG supervise elections and accounts across both tiers.
- **Security and Central Agencies:** **Article 355** requires the Union to protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance; **Entry 2A, List I** (42nd Amdt) enables CAPF deployment despite police being a State subject. Unilateral CAPF deployments and state withdrawals of **"general consent"** to the CBI (**Section 6, DSPE Act, 1946**) remain flashpoints, partly mediated by NITI Aayog and Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act, 1956).

## 5. Financial relations (Part XII, Articles 268–293)

Part XII (**Articles 268–293**) governs taxation, revenue sharing, grants-in-aid, borrowing, and tax immunity (the Finance Commission and GST Council are detailed in the Fiscal Federalism note).

### Taxing powers and revenue distribution (Articles 268–271)

Tax entries are strictly segregated from regulatory entries, with **no overlapping List I/II taxes** except **GST (Article 246A)**; residuary taxing power rests solely with Parliament. State taxation faces four ceilings: **Article 276** caps profession/trade tax at **₹2,500/year** (raised from ₹250 by the **60th Amendment, 1988**); **Article 286** bars state tax on out-of-state or import/export supplies; **Article 287** exempts Union/Railway electricity; and **Article 288** requires Presidential assent to tax inter-state river authorities.

Reshaped by the **80th Amendment (2000)** (pooling nearly all Union taxes per the 10th FC's "Alternative Scheme") and the **101st Amendment (2016)** (GST), revenue sharing runs across five Articles:

| Article | Levied by | Collected by | Shared / appropriated | Taxes covered (post-101st Amdt) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Art. 268** | Centre | **States** | **States** (outside CFI) | **Stamp duties** on bills of exchange, cheques, insurance, share transfers *(medicinal/toilet excise omitted by 101st Amdt)* |
| **Art. 269** | Centre | Centre | **Wholly to States** | Inter-state sale of non-GST goods (petroleum, liquor) & **consignment** taxes |
| **Art. 269A** | Centre | Centre | **Centre & States** (per **GST Council**) | **IGST** on inter-state supplies and imports |
| **Art. 270** | Centre | Centre | **Divisible Pool** (per **Finance Commission**) | Nearly all Union taxes (income, corporate, customs, excise, **CGST**, Union IGST share), **EXCEPT** Arts. 268/269/269A, **Art. 271 surcharges**, and **cesses** |
| **Art. 271** | Centre | Centre | **Exclusively Centre** | **Surcharge** on Arts. 269 & 270 taxes—**barred on GST (Art. 246A)** |

Under **Article 274**, **prior Presidential recommendation** is mandatory to introduce bills affecting state-interest taxes, "agricultural income", or Article 271 surcharges.

### Grants-in-aid: statutory (Article 275) versus discretionary (Article 282)

| Feature | Statutory Grants (**Article 275**) | Discretionary Grants (**Article 282**) |
|---|---|---|
| **Granting tier** | **Parliament** (Union → States) | **Both Centre and States** |
| **Basis** | States **"in need of assistance"**; **Finance Commission** advice | **"Any public purpose"**, even outside that tier's list |
| **Status & provisos** | **Charged** on CFI; mandatory provisos for **ST welfare/Scheduled Areas** & **Assam's 6th Schedule** | **Discretionary** & votable; basis of **Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS)** |

### Borrowing (Articles 292–293), tax immunity and emergencies

Under **Article 292**, the **Centre** borrows **within or outside India** on CFI security. Under **Article 293**, **States** borrow **only within India** and under **Article 293(3)** need **Union consent** (with conditions under Art. 293(4)) whenever any central loan or guarantee remains outstanding—giving the Centre leverage over annual Net Borrowing Ceilings.

> **Example:** In ***State of Kerala v. Union of India*** (**1 April 2024**, `2024 INSC 253`), Kerala challenged the Union deducting state-enterprise off-budget borrowings and Public Account liabilities from its borrowing limit under **Article 293(3)**. Denying interim relief, the Supreme Court referred Article 293's interpretation to a **five-judge Constitution Bench**.

Under **Article 285**, **Union property** (sovereign or commercial, though *not* separate PSU corporations) is exempt from state/local taxes unless Parliament allows. Under **Article 289**, **State property and income** are exempt from Union *direct* taxes—**not** indirect taxes like customs or excise (***In re Sea Customs Act***, 1963), and Parliament may tax state **commercial ventures** unless declared incidental to ordinary governance. During emergencies, **Article 354** (National Emergency) lets the President alter Articles 268–279 revenue distribution up to that financial year's end (laid before Parliament), while **Article 360** (Financial Emergency) enables financial propriety canons, salary cuts (including SC/HC judges), and Money Bill reservation.

## 6. Contemporary strains and the way forward

Four pressure points test Parts XI and XII today: shrinking state legislative space (Concurrent List expansion and the 2026 MMDR Amendment curtailing *MADA*), gubernatorial delays under Articles 200–201, administrative friction over AIS deputations, CAPFs and CBI consent, and fiscal centralisation via non-shareable cesses, Article 282 schemes, and Article 293 caps. Both the **Sarkaria (1988)** and **Punchhi (2010)** Commissions urged institutional fixes within the Constitution: pre-legislative consultation on Concurrent bills, regular quarterly sittings of the **Inter-State Council (Article 263)**, codified restraint in bill assent, and rule-based fiscal transfers.

> **Mains angle:** Frame GS-2 answers across three pillars: (1) **Constitutional logic** (Parts XI–XII balancing autonomy and cohesion); (2) **Live fault lines**—legislative (*MADA* 2024 vs MMDR Amdt 2026, *Lalta Prasad Vaish* 2024, Arts. 200–201 *Special Reference* 2025), administrative (Art. 258, Art. 365, CBI consent), and financial (Art. 271, Arts. 275/282, Art. 293 *Kerala* suit); and (3) **Way forward** via Sarkaria/Punchhi institutional safeguards.

- **Art. 245:** Only Parliament makes **extra-territorial laws**; states need **territorial nexus** (*Chamarbaugwala*, 1957). Exceptions: **Art. 240** UTs (Puducherry only if Assembly suspended/dissolved), **5th Schedule** (Governor), **6th Schedule** (Governor for Assam; **President** for Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram).

- **42nd Amdt (1976):** Shifted **5 subjects** to List III—**Education, Forests, Weights & measures, Wild animals & birds, Administration of justice (lower courts)**.
- **Arts. 246A & 248:** **Art. 246A** confers simultaneous GST power (Parliament exclusive on inter-state supply/imports); **Art. 248 + Entry 97 List I** give **residuary powers** to Parliament (Canada model).
- **Doctrines:** **Pith & Substance** (*Balsara*, 1951); **Colourable Legislation** (competence, not motive—*Gajapati Narayan Deo*, 1953); **Harmonious Construction**; **Repugnancy (Art. 254)**—Central law prevails except **Art. 254(2)** assented State law, still repealable by Parliament.

Lalta Prasad Vaish*** (Oct 2024, 9J, 8:1)—**industrial alcohol** under Entry 8 List II.
- **Parliament on List II:** **Art. 249** (RS **2/3 present & voting**, 1 yr renewable, ends 6 mo after); **Art. 250** (Emergency, ends 6 mo after); **Art. 252** (2+ States—**only Parliament** amends/repeals); **Art. 253** (treaties); **Art. 356** (President's Rule—law **survives**).
- **State bills:** **Arts. 200–201** (President's absolute veto); **Art. 304(b)** (prior sanction); **Art. 360** (Money Bills). ***Special Reference No. 1 of 2025*** (Nov 2025, 5J) barred rigid timelines & "deemed assent" (*Tamil Nadu*, Apr 2025), allowing limited scrutiny of indefinite inaction.
- **Executive power (Arts. 73 & 162):** Co-extensive with legislation; **Concurrent List** execution ordinarily stays with **States**.
- **Directions:** **Art. 256** (Union laws), **Art. 257** (military communications, railways), **Art. 339(2)** (ST welfare), **Art. 350A** (mother tongue); breach triggers **Art. 365** → **Art. 356**.
- **Delegation:** **Art. 258(1)** (President → State *with* consent); **Art. 258(2)** (Parliament → State *without* consent); **Art. 258A** (7th Amdt 1956: Governor → Centre *with* consent).

- **Tax caps:** No List I/II overlap except **GST (Art. 246A)**; **Art. 276** profession tax cap **₹2,500/yr** (60th Amdt 1988); **Art. 286** bars out-of-state/import/export state taxes.
- **Tax sharing (Arts. 268–271):** **Art. 268** (stamp duties → States collect/keep); **Art. 269** (non-GST inter-state/consignment → States); **Art. 269A** (IGST → GST Council); **Art. 270** (divisible pool, **80th Amdt 2000**, excludes Art. 271 surcharges & cesses); **Art. 271** (surcharge **barred on GST**); **Art. 274** (prior Presidential recommendation).

- **Immunity & Emergencies:** **Art. 285** exempts Union property (not separate PSUs); **Art. 289** exempts State property/income from Union *direct* taxes—**not** customs/excise (*Sea Customs Act*, 1963) or taxable businesses. **Art. 354** alters Arts. 268–279 in Emergency; **Art. 360** enables salary cuts & Money Bill reservation.
