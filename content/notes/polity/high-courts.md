## 1. High Courts: origin, organisation and composition

At the apex of each state's judicial administration sits the **High Court**, governed by **Part VI, Chapter V (Articles 214–231)**. Originating under the **Indian High Courts Act, 1861**, the first three opened at **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras (1862)**, followed by **Allahabad (1866)**.

**Number and common High Courts:** As of **November 2026**, India has **25 High Courts**—the newest being separate **Andhra Pradesh** and **Telangana** High Courts (effective **1 January 2019**). While **Article 214** prescribes a High Court per state, the **7th Amendment Act, 1956 (Article 231)** lets **Parliament by law** create a **common High Court** for two or more states, or states and UTs: **Gauhati** (four states—Assam, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh), **Bombay** (Maharashtra, Goa, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu), **Punjab & Haryana** (+ Chandigarh), **Madras** (+ Puducherry), **Calcutta** (+ Andaman & Nicobar), and **Kerala** (+ Lakshadweep). Among UTs, only **Delhi (1966)** and **Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh** (a common HC for both UTs) have their own High Courts. Parliament may also extend or exclude HC jurisdiction over any UT (**Art 230**).

**Strength (Article 216):** Unlike the Supreme Court (fixed by Parliament), HC strength is **not fixed by the Constitution**; the **President** determines it from time to time. As of **1 November 2026** (Department of Justice), the 25 High Courts have **1,122 sanctioned posts** (847 permanent + 275 additional), **827 working judges**, and **295 vacancies (26.3%)**—from **Allahabad** (160 sanctioned) down to **Sikkim** (3).

### Appointment, qualifications, tenure, removal and funds

- **Appointment (Art 217(1)):** By the **President** after consulting the **CJI**, **State Governor**, and (for non-CJ judges) the **HC Chief Justice**. Under *Third Judges Case* (1998), the SC Collegium for HCs is **CJI + 2 seniormost SC judges** (consulting SC judges conversant with that HC).
- **Qualifications (Art 217(2)):** **Indian citizen** + (a) **10 years judicial office** in India, OR (b) **10 years HC advocate**. **NO "distinguished jurist" clause** and **NO minimum age**.
- **Oath, tenure and removal:** Oath by the **State Governor** (Third Schedule, **Art 219**). Retires at **62 years** (raised from 60 by **15th Amendment, 1963**; **President** decides age disputes after consulting CJI—**final**, **Art 217(3)**). Resigns to the **President**; removed identically to an SC judge (Parliament special majority in both Houses, same session + Presidential order—**Governor has NO role**).
- **Salary vs Pension trap & practice bar:** Salaries/allowances are **charged on the State Consolidated Fund** (**Art 202(3)(d)**), **BUT PENSIONS are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India** (**Art 112(3)(d)(iii)**). Under **Article 220**, a retired permanent HC judge may plead **ONLY in the SC and OTHER High Courts**, never in their permanent HC or lower courts.

### Transfer and temporary High Court judges (Articles 222–224A)

- **Transfer (Art 222):** By the **President** after consulting the **CJI** (**CJI + 4 seniormost SC judges** + Chief Justices of both HCs; *Third Judges Case*, 1998); judicial review lies only for non-consultation, initiated solely by the transferred judge.
- **Acting CJ (Art 223)** and **Additional/Acting Judges (Art 224):** Appointed by the **President**—Additional judges for up to **two years** to clear arrears, Acting judges for temporary absence; neither serves past **62**.
- **Retired Judges (Art 224A, 15th Amendment 1963):** HC Chief Justice requests a retired HC judge **with the President's previous consent** and the judge's consent; receives President-fixed allowances and full HC powers, yet **not deemed an HC judge**.

> **In the news:** **Article 224A ad hoc judges relaxed (30 January 2025).** Having activated dormant Art 224A in ***Lok Prahari* (April 2021)** with a >20% vacancy floor, a three-judge SC Bench (CJI Sanjiv Khanna, Justices B.R. Gavai and Surya Kant) kept that floor in abeyance on **30 January 2025**, letting each High Court recommend **2 to 5** ad hoc retired judges (capped at **10%** of sanctioned strength) to sit with a sitting judge on Division Benches primarily to clear **criminal appeals**.

> **Prelims pointer:** Five HC traps: (1) **President** (not Parliament) fixes HC strength; (2) **Governor** gives oath (Art 219), but **President** accepts resignation & issues removal (**Governor has zero removal role**); (3) **salary** from **State CF**, **pension** from **CFI**; (4) **NO "distinguished jurist"** route; and (5) retired permanent HC judges may practise only in **SC and OTHER HCs** (Art 220).

## 2. Jurisdiction and powers of High Courts

- **Original jurisdiction:** Admiralty, marriage, divorce, company law, contempt, **MP/MLA election petitions**, and revenue; **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras and Delhi** HCs also hold original civil jurisdiction above monetary limits.
- **Writ jurisdiction (Art 226):** Wider than Art 32—enforces Fundamental Rights AND **"for any other purpose"** (legal rights)—yet **discretionary**. Under the **15th Amendment (1963)** (**Art 226(2)**), writs extend outside state territory if the **cause of action** arises wholly/partly within it. Declared part of **Basic Structure** in ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** (tribunals remain subject to HC Division Bench review under Arts 226/227).
- **Appellate & Supervisory (Art 227):** Hears civil/criminal appeals and **must confirm every Sessions Court death sentence** before execution. **Article 227** confers both **judicial and administrative superintendence** over **all courts and tribunals** within territory **EXCEPT Armed Forces courts/tribunals**—wider than *certiorari* and usable *suo motu*.
- **Other powers:** Subordinate-court control (**Art 235**), **Court of Record (Art 215)** (evidentiary value + contempt), withdrawal of subordinate cases involving substantial constitutional questions (**Art 228**), and **judicial review** over State and Central laws (restored by **43rd Amendment, 1977** after 42nd Amendment curbs).

| Feature | Supreme Court (Part V) | High Court (Part VI) |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength fixed by** | **Parliament** by ordinary law (**38** in 2026) | **President** from time to time (**1,122** total) |
| **Qualifications** | 5 yrs HC judge / 10 yrs HC advocate / **"distinguished jurist"** | 10 yrs judicial office / 10 yrs HC advocate (**NO distinguished jurist**) |
| **Oath / Resignation** | President / President | **Governor** / **President** |
| **Retirement / Age dispute** | **65 yrs** / Parliament's law (Art 124(2A)) | **62 yrs** / **President** + CJI—final (Art 217(3)) |
| **Salary & Pension** | Both charged on **Consolidated Fund of India** | Salary on **State CF**; **Pension on CFI** (Art 112(3)(d)) |
| **Post-retirement bar** | Barred in **all** Indian courts/authorities (Art 124(7)) | Allowed in **SC and OTHER HCs** only (Art 220) |
| **Writ scope** | **Art 32:** FRs only; itself a FR (cannot refuse) | **Art 226:** FRs + **"any other purpose"**; discretionary |
| **Superintendence** | No general administrative superintendence over HCs | **Art 227:** Judicial + administrative (except military) |

## 3. Subordinate courts: constitutional framework and hierarchy

**Part VI, Chapter VI (Articles 233–237)** governs the district judiciary—where over **88%** of India's cases are tried.

- **District Judges (Art 233):** Appointed, posted and promoted by the **Governor in consultation with the High Court** (binding consultation per ***Chandra Mohan*, 1966**; **Art 236** defines District Judge broadly to include Sessions Judges, City Civil Judges and Additional/Assistant District Judges). Under **Art 233(2)**, external direct recruits need **>= 7 years** as an advocate/pleader and HC recommendation.
- **Other judicial officers (Art 234):** Appointed by the **Governor** after consulting the **SPSC** and the **High Court**.
- **Control (Art 235):** Posting, promotion, leave and discipline over subordinate courts vest exclusively in the **High Court**, operationalising **Article 50** (separation from the executive). (**Art 237** allows extending Ch VI to notified magistrates.)

> **In the news:** **Two 2025 Supreme Court verdicts on subordinate judicial recruitment.**
> 1. ***Rejanish K.V. v. K. Deepa* (9 October 2025, 5-judge Constitution Bench led by CJI B.R. Gavai):** Unanimously overruled *Dheeraj Mor* (2020), holding under **Article 233** that serving judicial officers with **7 years' combined experience** as an advocate and/or judicial officer (assessed on the **date of application**, with **35 years minimum age** for both Bar and in-service candidates, prospective effect) are eligible for **direct recruitment** as District/Additional District Judges.
> 2. ***All India Judges Association* (20 May 2025, 3-judge Bench led by CJI B.R. Gavai):** Overturning its 2002 order, restored **minimum 3 years of legal practice** (from provisional Bar enrolment; judicial clerkship counts) for **Civil Judge (Junior Division)** exams, while lifting Higher Judicial Service LDCE promotion quota to **25%** and introducing a **10%** LDCE quota for Civil Judge (Senior Division).

Every district runs twin civil and criminal ladders headed by the **same officer**—**District Judge** (civil) and **Sessions Judge** (criminal):

Under **Section 23, BNSS 2023** (w.e.f. **1 July 2024**, replacing CrPC Sec 29), a **CJM** passes sentences up to **7 years** (excluding death/life); a **JMFC** up to **3 years**, fine raised to **₹50,000** (from ₹10,000), or **community service**; and a **Second Class Magistrate** up to **1 year**, fine up to **₹10,000**, or **community service**. (Grassroots and alternative fora—**Lok Adalats, Gram Nyayalayas, Family Courts and Tribunals**—are covered in the companion note on tribunals and ADR.)

## 4. Pendency and structural bottlenecks

Delayed justice undermines **Articles 21 and 39A** across every tier of the system:

| Indicator | Verified Figure | Source & Period |
|---|---|---|
| **Total pending cases** | **~5.85 cr+** (5.19 cr District; 65.58 lakh HCs; 92,755+ SC) | NJDG (3 Nov 2026); LS reply (Jul 2026) |
| **High Court vacancies** | **295 of 1,122** (**26.3%**); ~7,930 cases per working judge | Dept. of Justice (1 Nov 2026) |
| **District judiciary vacancies** | **7,310 of 30,868** (**23.7%**) | Nyaya Vikas Portal 2.0 (3 Aug 2026) |
| **Judge-to-population ratio** | **~22 per million** (vs 120th Law Comm. target of **50**) | Parliament records (2025) |
| **Undertrial prisoners** | **72.5%–72.6%** of all inmates (~3.7 lakh of ~5.1 lakh) | NCRB PSI 2024 (rel. 2026) |
| **Judiciary spending** | **₹182/capita** (vs ₹1,275 police); **no state >1%** of budget | India Justice Report (2025) |

> **Remember:** While citing the **120th Law Commission's (1987)** **50 judges per million** target, note for Mains that the **245th Law Commission (2014)** and **NCMS Committee (2016)** favoured a **"weighted rate of disposal"** metric because filings vary widely across states.

**Five root causes:** (1) **Chronic vacancies** despite ***Malik Mazhar Sultan* (2007)** recruitment timelines; (2) **Government as the largest litigant**—Union, states and PSUs historically account for ~**46–50%** of court dockets through mechanical appeals (the Union's **LIMBS** portal alone tracked **7.27 lakh** Central cases in March 2025); (3) **Adjournment culture**, weak case management and frequent interlocutory appeals; (4) **Budget and infrastructure deficit** (~0.1% of GDP combined; states spend ~80% of justice budgets on police vs ~15–20% on courts, IJR Nov 2025); and (5) **Docket flooding by special laws** (cheque-bounce cases under **Sec 138 NI Act**, motor-accident claims and traffic challans).

## 5. Key judicial reforms and institutional debates

### 5.1 Digital justice: e-Courts Phase III and judicial AI

Approved on **13 September 2023** with **₹7,210 crore for 2023–27**, **e-Courts Phase III** builds paperless courts, cloud registries, Virtual Courts, live-streaming and **e-Sewa Kendras**. By **March 2026** (Lok Sabha reply), **660.36 crore pages** were digitised, **3.97+ crore** video-conferencing hearings held, and **2,444 e-Sewa Kendras** operational. Three Supreme Court tools reinforce this ecosystem: **FASTER** (instant authenticated electronic transmission of bail/court orders to prisons), **SUVAS** (AI vernacular translation of judgments), and **SUPACE** (AI legal research assistant organising case files).

### 5.2 The All India Judicial Service (AIJS) debate (Article 312)

Enabled by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**, **Article 312(1)** lets Parliament create an **AIJS** via a **Rajya Sabha resolution** passed by **>= two-thirds present and voting**, followed by an ordinary Act (NOT Art 368—**Art 312(4)**). Crucially, **Article 312(3)** bars AIJS from including any post **inferior to a District Judge**.

| Arguments FOR AIJS | Arguments AGAINST AIJS |
|---|---|
| **Merit & uniform quality:** National exam attracts top talent (**1st, 14th & 116th Law Commissions**, **NITI Aayog**). | **Local language barrier:** District trials, witnesses and land records use regional languages. |
| **Faster vacancy filling:** Regular calendar ends erratic state recruitment delays. | **Federalism & Art 235 control:** Erodes High Court superintendence and state administrative authority. |
| **Social diversity:** Uniform reservation broadens SC, ST, OBC and women pipelines to HCs. | **Promotion bottleneck:** Shrinks career progression of state Civil Judges—drawing opposition from most HCs and States. |

### 5.3 Regional Benches of the Supreme Court / National Court of Appeal

The **229th Law Commission Report (2009)** proposed making Delhi a dedicated **Constitutional Court** supported by **four regional Cassation Benches** (North, South, East, West) to curb travel costs and let Delhi focus on constitutional law. Though **Article 130** empowers the CJI with President's approval to notify seats, the **SC Full Court** has repeatedly opposed regional benches citing institutional unity; a 2016 Constitution Bench reference on a **National Court of Appeal** remains pending, while hybrid hearings partially bridge distance.

### 5.4 Government litigation, FTSCs and institutional overhaul

- **April 2025 Litigation Directive:** Dropping a Cabinet-level National Litigation Policy, the Department of Legal Affairs issued the **"Directive for the Efficient and Effective Management of Litigation by the Government of India"** (**4 April 2025**) for all Union Ministries, Departments and CPSEs—tracked via **LIMBS** (a Parliamentary Standing Committee nonetheless selected NLP review for 2026–27).
- **Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs):** Launched October 2019 (Nirbhaya Fund, Centrally Sponsored Scheme, extended to Sept 2026) for rape and POCSO trials; **779 FTSCs** (including **399 exclusive e-POCSO courts**) across 29 States/UTs disposed of **3.97 lakh cases** up to **30 June 2026**.
- **Infrastructure, Case Management & ADR:** Proposed **National Judicial Infrastructure Authority of India (NJIAI)** (ex-CJI N.V. Ramana), strict adjournment caps, **Evening Courts**, and mandatory **pre-institution mediation** (**Mediation Act, 2023**; Commercial Courts Act—detailed in the companion note on tribunals and ADR).

> **Mains angle:** Structure a **four-pillar Mains conclusion** on pendency: (1) **Capacity**—fill ~24–26% vacancies via time-bound MoP and *Malik Mazhar Sultan* calendars, lift state court budgets above 1%, and deploy **Art 224A** ad hoc judges; (2) **Litigation reduction**—enforce the **April 2025 Litigation Directive**, decriminalise petty offences, and scale **Mediation Act, 2023** & Lok Adalats; (3) **Process & Tech**—complete **e-Courts Phase III**, cap adjournments, and enforce BNSS timelines; and (4) **Accountability**—extend the SC's **2025 website asset-disclosure** norm to High Courts with transparent performance audits.

- **High Courts (Arts 214–231):** First three under **Indian High Courts Act, 1861**—Calcutta, Bombay, Madras (1862); Allahabad (1866). **25 HCs** (Nov 2026; AP & Telangana split **1 Jan 2019**).
- **Common HCs & UTs (Arts 230–231):** **7th Amendment (1956)** enabled common HCs—**Gauhati (4 states)**, Bombay, Punjab & Haryana, Madras, Calcutta, Kerala. Only **Delhi (1966)** and **J&K and Ladakh** among UTs have their own HCs.
- **Strength (Art 216):** Fixed by **President** (NOT Parliament—contrast SC). As of **1 Nov 2026** (DoJ): **1,122** sanctioned, **827** working, **295 vacant (26.3%)**.
- **Appointment & Qualifications (Art 217):** President + CJI + Governor + HC CJ (**CJI + 2** Collegium). Citizen + 10 yrs judicial office OR 10 yrs HC advocate; **NO "distinguished jurist"** & **NO minimum age**.
- **Oath, Tenure, Removal & Funds:** Oath by **Governor** (Art 219); retires at **62** (**15th Amendment, 1963**; President decides age disputes after CJI—final); resigns to **President**; removed like SC judge (**Governor has NO role**). **Salary** on **State CF**, **PENSION on CFI** (**Art 112(3)(d)**).
- **Practice bar & Transfer:** Retired permanent HC judge pleads **only in SC & OTHER HCs** (**Art 220**). **Transfer (Art 222):** President + CJI (**CJI + 4** + both HC CJs); review only by judge for non-consultation.
- **Temporary Judges (Arts 223–224A):** Acting CJ (223), Additional/Acting (224: max 2 yrs, up to 62), Retired (**224A**: HC CJ + President's prior consent). **30 Jan 2025 SC order (*Lok Prahari*)** suspended >20% vacancy floor, permitting **2–5** ad hoc retired judges (max **10%** strength) on Division Benches for **criminal appeals**.
- **Writ & Superintendence:** **Art 226** wider than Art 32 (FRs + **"any other purpose"**; discretionary; cause-of-action reach via 15th Amendment; Basic Structure per ***L. Chandra Kumar*, 1997**). **Art 227** = judicial + administrative superintendence over all courts/tribunals **except Armed Forces**. Also **Court of Record (215)**, withdrawal (**228**), death confirmation.
- **Subordinate Courts (Arts 233–237):** **District Judges (233):** Governor + binding HC consultation; direct recruits need **>=7 yrs** Bar experience. **Other officers (234):** Governor + SPSC + HC. **Control (235):** Vests in **High Court** (realises **Art 50**).
- **2025 District Judiciary Rulings:** ***Rejanish K.V. v. K. Deepa* (9 Oct 2025, 5 judges)** overruled *Dheeraj Mor*—judicial officers with **7 yrs combined Bar + judicial experience** (min age **35**) eligible for direct District Judge recruitment under **Art 233**. ***AIJA* (20 May 2025)** restored **3-year Bar practice** for **Civil Judge (Jr. Div.)** exams & raised HJS LDCE quota to 25%.
- **Criminal Hierarchy (Section 23, BNSS 2023):** Sessions Judge (any sentence; death needs HC confirmation) → **CJM** (<= **7 yrs**) → **JMFC** (<= **3 yrs**, fine up to **₹50,000**, or **community service**) → **JMSC** (<= 1 yr, ₹10,000, or community service).
- **Pendency & Capacity (2025–2026):** **~5.85 cr+** pending (NJDG, Nov 2026: 5.19 cr district, 65.58 lakh HCs, 92,755+ SC). District vacancies: **7,310 of 30,868 (23.7%)** (Aug 2026). **~22 judges/million** vs 120th Law Comm.'s **50** (245th Report & NCMS prefer weighted disposal).
- **Undertrials & Spending:** **72.5%–72.6%** of inmates are undertrials (**NCRB 2024**, rel. 2026). Judiciary spend is **₹182/capita**; **no state >1%** of budget (**India Justice Report 2025**).
- **Root causes:** Vacancies (despite *Malik Mazhar Sultan*, 2007), **government as biggest litigant** (~46–50% total; 7.27 lakh Union cases on LIMBS, Mar 2025), adjournments, weak infrastructure, special-law flooding (Sec 138 NI Act).
- **e-Courts Phase III (2023–27, ₹7,210 cr):** **660.36 cr pages** digitised, **3.97+ cr** VC hearings, **2,444 e-Sewa Kendras** (LS reply, Mar 2026); plus **FASTER**, **SUVAS** & **SUPACE**.
- **AIJS (Art 312, 42nd Amendment 1976):** Needs **Rajya Sabha 2/3rd present & voting** resolution + ordinary Act; **District Judge level and above ONLY** (**Art 312(3)**). Pros: merit, diversity, speed; Cons: local language, federalism, Art 235 control, civil-judge promotion loss.
- **SC Regional Benches:** **229th Law Commission (2009)** proposed Constitutional Court in Delhi + **4 Cassation Benches**; opposed by SC Full Court citing institutional unity.
- **Litigation Directive & FTSCs:** **4 April 2025 Directive** for Union Ministries/CPSEs replaced Cabinet NLP plan (monitored via LIMBS). **779 functional FTSCs** (**399 e-POCSO**) across 29 States/UTs disposed **3.97 lakh cases** (30 June 2026).
- **Mains Way Forward:** Fill vacancies & deploy Art 224A; curb state litigation & scale **Mediation Act, 2023**; finish **e-Courts Phase III** & NJIAI-style infrastructure; extend 2025 asset-disclosure accountability to HCs.
