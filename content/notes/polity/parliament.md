## 1. Structure, composition and duration (Articles 79–83)

Part V (**Articles 79 to 122**) builds India's Union legislature on Westminster executive–legislative interdependence. Under **Article 79**, Parliament comprises **three** organs—the **President**, the **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)** and the **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**. Though not a member of either House, the President is integral to Parliament: Bills require Presidential assent (Article 111), and the President summons, prorogues and addresses Parliament and dissolves the Lok Sabha.

```text
             ┌──────────────────────────────┐
             │      PARLIAMENT (Art 79)     │
             └──────────────┬───────────────┘
        ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┐
        ↓                   ↓                   ↓
┌───────────────┐   ┌───────────────┐   ┌───────────────┐
│   PRESIDENT   │   │  RAJYA SABHA  │   │   LOK SABHA   │
│  (Integral,   │   │ Max: 250      │   │ Max: 550      │
│  not a member)│   │ Now: 245      │   │ Now: 543      │
│  Assents,     │   │ 225 States    │   │ 524 States    │
│  summons,     │   │   8 UTs       │   │  19 UTs       │
│  dissolves LS │   │  12 Nominated │   │ (0 Nominated) │
└───────────────┘   └───────────────┘   └───────────────┘
```

### Rajya Sabha (Article 80)

Maximum strength is **250** (238 elected + 12 nominated); **current strength is 245**: **225** from 28 States, **8** from three Union Territories with Assemblies—**Jammu and Kashmir (4)** (filled in **October 2025** after the 2024 Assembly polls), **NCT of Delhi (3)** and **Puducherry (1)**—and **12 nominated** by the President. Unlike the US Senate's equal two seats per state, India's **Fourth Schedule** allocates seats **by population** (Uttar Pradesh 31; Goa, Sikkim and six northeastern states 1 each).

State representatives are elected **indirectly** by **elected MLAs** through **proportional representation by single transferable vote (PR-STV)** (**Article 80(4)**). A **2003 amendment to the Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1951** introduced **open ballot** (party MLAs show marked ballots to an authorised party agent) and removed state domicile; both were upheld in ***Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India* (2006)**. In ***Shailesh Manubhai Parmar v. ECI* (2018)**, the Court barred **NOTA** in Rajya Sabha polls, warning it encourages defection. Under **Article 80(3)**, the President nominates **12 members** from **four fields**: **literature, science, art and social service**.

> **Prelims pointer:** Article 80(3) lists only **four** fields—**NO "cooperative movement"** (which applies only to State MLCs under Article 171(5)). **NOTA does not apply** to Rajya Sabha polls, and open ballot binds party MLAs (not independents).

### Lok Sabha: seats, delimitation and reservations (Articles 81–82, 330–334A)

Directly elected via **first-past-the-post (FPTP)** on **universal adult suffrage (Article 326)**, the Lok Sabha's **maximum strength is 550** (530 States + 20 UTs)—down from 552 after the **104th Amendment Act, 2019** (effective January 2020) ended two **Anglo-Indian** nominations (**Article 331**). Current strength is **543**: **524** from States and **19** from UTs (Delhi 7, J&K 5, Ladakh 1).

**Article 81(2)** pursues uniform seat-to-population ratios across states (exempting states under **60 lakh** population) and within each state. Delimitation Commissions (**Article 82**) operated in 1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002. To avoid penalising states achieving population control, the **42nd Amendment (1976)** and **84th Amendment (2001)** froze inter-state seat totals at the **1971 Census** until the **first census after 2026**, while the **87th Amendment (2003)** allowed **internal** constituency redrawing on the **2001 Census**.

Under **Article 330**, **131 seats** are reserved—**84 SC** and **47 ST** (extended to **2030** by the 104th Amendment; no separate electorates). The **106th Amendment Act, 2023** (*Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*) inserted **Articles 330A, 332A and 334A** and amended **Article 239AA** to reserve **one-third of seats for women** in the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and Delhi Assembly (including sub-quotas within SC/ST seats, **not** in Rajya Sabha or Councils) for **15 years** on rotation.

> **In the news:** **Delimitation, Women's Quota and One Nation, One Election (Nov 2026).**
> - **106th Amendment notified (16 April 2026):** Commenced **16 April 2026**, yet **Article 334A** ties activation to delimitation after **Census 2027** (House Listing began **1 April 2026**; Population Enumeration set for **February 2027**, reference date **1 March 2027**).
> - **131st Amendment Bill, 2026 defeated (17 April 2026):** Introduced **16 April 2026** to lift the 1971 freeze, delimit on the **2011 Census**, expand Lok Sabha to **850 seats** (815 States + 35 UTs), and fast-track women's reservation, the Bill **failed** in a Lok Sabha division (**298–230** out of 528—short of Article 368's two-thirds mark) amid Southern and Northeastern objections; companion Delimitation and UT Laws Bills were withdrawn.
> - **129th Amendment Bill, 2024 (One Nation, One Election):** Introduced **17 December 2024** (inserts **Art 82A**, amends **Arts 83, 172, 327**; mid-term houses serve only the **unexpired term**), the Bill and companion UT Bill sit with a 41-member **JPC** (P.P. Chaudhary) extended until the **last week of Winter Session 2026**.

### Duration (Article 83)

| Feature | Rajya Sabha (Art 83(1)) | Lok Sabha (Art 83(2)) |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity** | **Permanent**; never dissolved | Dissolves after **5 years** (or earlier) |
| **Member term** | **6 years**; 1/3rd retire every 2 years | 5 years from first sitting |
| **Legal source** | **RPA, 1951 (s. 154)** fixes 6 yrs—**NOT Constitution!** | **Constitution** directly fixes 5 yrs |
| **Emergency rule** | Not applicable | Extendable **1 yr at a time** during Art 352 Emergency; lapses max **6 months** post-Emergency |

## 2. Membership: qualifications, disqualifications and vacation (Articles 84, 88, 99, 101–104)

**Qualifications (Article 84 & RPA, 1951):** Indian citizen; Third Schedule oath; minimum age **30 (RS)** or **25 (LS)**; registered as an **elector in any parliamentary constituency** in India (2003 dropped RS state-domicile—*Kuldip Nayar*, 2006). SC/ST candidates may contest reserved or general seats.

**Disqualifications (Articles 102–103 & Tenth Schedule):** **Article 102(1)** disqualifies for (a) **office of profit** under Union/State governments (Ministers exempt under the **Explanation to Article 102(1)**; others under the **Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act, 1959**), (b) court-declared unsound mind, (c) undischarged insolvency, (d) non-citizenship, or (e) parliamentary law—chiefly **RPA, 1951** (corrupt practices, **two-year conviction under Section 8(3)** barring candidacy for sentence + **6 years**, election-account default, government-contract interest, >=25% PSU office, corruption dismissal, or social crimes).

The Constitution **does not define "office of profit"**: in ***Jaya Bachchan v. Union of India* (2006)**, the Court ruled **capability to yield pecuniary gain**—not actual receipt—triggers disqualification. In ***Lily Thomas v. Union of India* (2013)**, the Court struck down **Section 8(4) of the RPA, 1951** (three-month appellate shield), making two-year conviction trigger **immediate disqualification** unless the **conviction itself** is stayed (*Lok Prahari*, 2018).

> **Prelims pointer:** **Article 102(1) / RPA 1951** disqualifications are decided by the **President**, bound by the **ECI's opinion** (**Article 103**). **Tenth Schedule** defection disqualifications (**Article 102(2)**) are decided by the **Speaker or Chairman**, subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992; see *Parliament IV*).

**Vacation of seats (Article 101):**
- **Double membership (RPA 1951 & 1950 Rules):** Both Houses → choose in **10 days**, else **RS seat** lapses (s. 68); sitting MP elected to other House → **first House seat** lapses immediately (s. 69); **two seats in one House** → resign one in **14 days**, else **both** lapse; **Parliament + State Legislature** → resign state seat in **14 days**, else **Parliament seat** lapses!
- **Resignation (Art 101(3)(b)):** Written to Chairman/Speaker; after the **33rd Amendment, 1974**, the Chair may reject a resignation found **not voluntary or genuine**.
- **Absence (Art 101(4)):** House may vacate a seat after **60 days' unpermitted absence** (excluding prorogation or adjournments >**4 consecutive days**).
- **Disqualification, void election, expulsion, or election as President/VP/Governor.**

**Oath and ministerial rights:** MPs take the Third Schedule oath before the **President** (or appointee) (**Article 99**); sitting/voting beforehand incurs **₹500/day** (**Article 104**). Under **Article 88**, **Union Ministers** and the **Attorney General** may **speak and participate** in either House, joint sittings and committees of which they are members, **without voting** where non-members.

## 3. Presiding officers, parliamentary leaders and Secretariat (Articles 89–98)

### Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Articles 93–96)

Originating under the Government of India Act, 1919 (Sir Frederick Whyte, 1921; first elected Indian President of the Central Legislative Assembly, **Vithalbhai Patel**, 1925), the **Speaker** is elected by the Lok Sabha from its members on a date **fixed by the President** (**Article 93**). On **26 June 2024**, **Om Birla** won re-election by voice vote over Congress MP Kodikunnil Suresh—only the **fourth contested Speaker election** since 1952 (after 1952, 1967 and 1976).

> "The Speaker represents the House. He represents the dignity of the House, the freedom of the House and because the House represents the nation, in a particular way, the Speaker becomes the symbol of the nation's freedom and liberty. Therefore, it is right that that should be an honoured position, a free position and should be occupied always by men of outstanding ability and impartiality." — Jawaharlal Nehru, unveiling Vithalbhai Patel's portrait, 8 March 1948

**Tenure and removal (Articles 94, 96):** **Dissolution of the Lok Sabha does NOT vacate the Speaker's office**—the Speaker stays until **immediately before the first sitting of the new Lok Sabha**. Crucially (a classic **Prelims 2025** trap), **the Constitution does NOT require a Speaker to resign from their political party** upon election—resigning party membership is purely a British convention, though Tenth Schedule Paragraph 5 exempts a Speaker who voluntarily does so. Earlier vacation occurs only by ceasing to be an MP, resigning in writing to the **Deputy Speaker** (not the President), or **removal** by an **effective majority** (majority of *all the then members*) after **14 days' notice**. Under **Article 96**, during a removal resolution the Speaker **cannot preside**, yet may **speak, participate and vote in the first instance**—**NOT a casting vote**!

> **In the news:** **Removal motion against Speaker Om Birla (11 March 2026).** Signed by 118 Opposition MPs alleging partisanship, the resolution was **defeated by voice vote on 11 March 2026** after 13 hours of debate. With the **Deputy Speaker's post vacant since May 2019**, BJP MP **Jagdambika Pal** (Panel of Chairpersons) presided—exposing procedural uncertainty when Article 93's second chair stays empty.

**Powers and independence:** The Speaker maintains decorum, acts as **final interpreter** of the Constitution and Rules inside the House, suspends sittings lacking quorum, exercises a **casting vote only in a tie (Art 100(1))**, **certifies Money Bills (Art 110(3))**, presides over **Joint Sittings (Art 108)**, adjudicates **Tenth Schedule** petitions, allows secret sittings, appoints Lok Sabha committee chairs, and chairs the **Business Advisory, Rules and General Purposes Committees**. Independence rests on effective-majority tenure security, salaries **charged on the CFI (Art 112(3)(b))**, non-discussable conduct except via substantive motion, **Article 122** immunity from courts, and **6th rank** in Precedence (with the CJI).

### Deputy Speaker, Panel of Chairpersons and Speaker pro tem

- **Deputy Speaker (Articles 93–95):** Elected on a date **fixed by the Speaker**; **not subordinate** to the Speaker; resigns to the Speaker; **automatically chairs any committee** joined. Despite the post-1996 Opposition convention, the office stayed **vacant throughout the 17th LS and remains vacant in the 18th LS as of November 2026** (over seven years since May 2019).
- **Panel of Chairpersons:** Up to **10 MPs** nominated by the Speaker (RS has **Panel of Vice-Chairpersons**); preside with full powers whenever Speaker and Deputy Speaker are **absent**.
- **Speaker pro tem (Article 95(1)):** Appointed and sworn in by the **President** before a new Lok Sabha's first sitting—conventionally the **senior-most MP**—to administer oaths and conduct the Speaker's election. Appointing seven-term BJP MP **Bhartruhari Mahtab** (consecutive terms) over eight-term Congress MP **Kodikunnil Suresh** (non-consecutive) sparked controversy in **June 2024**.

> **Prelims pointer:** The **Panel of Chairpersons** presides **ONLY on ABSENCE**, **NEVER when both offices are VACANT**! On vacancy, the **President appoints an MP** under Article 95(1).

### Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Articles 89–92)

The **Vice-President** (**C.P. Radhakrishnan** since **12 September 2025**) is ex-officio Chairman (**Articles 64, 89**), removable only via Article 67(b). The House elects a **Deputy Chairman** from its members (removable by RS effective majority, 14-day notice). On **17 April 2026**, **Harivansh Narayan Singh**—nominated by President Murmu after his elected term ended 9 April 2026—won an unprecedented **third consecutive term** unopposed, becoming the **first nominated MP ever** to serve as RS Deputy Chairman.

| Feature | Speaker (Lok Sabha) | Chairman (Rajya Sabha) |
|---|---|---|
| **House member?** | **Yes** | **No** (ex-officio Vice-President) |
| **Money Bill / Joint Sitting** | Certifies Money Bills; presides over Joint Sitting | **Neither** |
| **Vote during own removal** | **Votes in first instance** (Art 96(2)); no casting vote | **Cannot vote at all** (Art 92(2)) |
| **Resignation to** | Deputy Speaker | President (as Vice-President) |

### Leaders of House & Opposition, Whips and Secretariat

- **Leader of the House:** Recognised in **Rules** (not Constitution): **Narendra Modi** (LS) and **J.P. Nadda** (RS).
- **Leader of the Opposition (LoP):** Statutory under the **Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in Parliament Act, 1977** (**Cabinet Minister rank**; first recognised 1969; **10% seat convention** via Speaker's Direction 121—55 LS, 25 RS). After a 10-year LS gap (2014–2024), **Rahul Gandhi** became LoP (LS) on **9 June 2024**; **Mallikarjun Kharge** is LoP (RS, reappointed w.e.f. **26 June 2026**). LoPs sit on selection panels for **CVC, CIC, Lokpal, CBI Director, NHRC and ECs**.
- **Whip:** Purely a **parliamentary convention**—**neither in the Constitution, nor Rules, nor any statute**. Defying a whip triggers Tenth Schedule disqualification unless condoned within 15 days.
- **Secretariat (Article 98):** Separate, independent secretariat for each House headed by a **Secretary-General** appointed by its Presiding Officer.

## 4. Sessions, conduct of business, motions and devices (Articles 85–87, 100, 118–120)

### Summoning, endings, quorum and Special Address

Under **Article 85(1)**, the **President summons** Parliament with **no more than six months' gap** between sessions (minimum two yearly; customarily **Budget, Monsoon and Winter**). A **lame-duck session** is the final session of an outgoing Lok Sabha after a new one has been elected.

| Device | Authority | Ends what? | Effect on pending business |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adjournment** | **Presiding Officer** | A **sitting** (fixed return) | **None** |
| **Adjournment *sine die*** | **Presiding Officer** | Sitting **indefinitely** (Chair can recall before prorogation) | **None** |
| **Prorogation** (Art 85(2)) | **President** | A **session** (e.g., Monsoon 2026 ended *sine die* 13 Aug 2026 without immediate prorogation) | **Bills do NOT lapse** (Art 107(3)); pending **notices lapse** |
| **Dissolution** (Art 85(2)) | **President** | Life of **Lok Sabha** (RS permanent) | Notices, motions & specified Bills **lapse** (see *Parliament II*) |

**Quorum (Article 100(3))** is **one-tenth of total membership including the Presiding Officer** (**55 LS, 25 RS**). **Article 120** designates **Hindi or English** (with mother-tongue permission from the Chair). Under **Article 87**, the President delivers a **Special Address** at the first session post-general election and the first session each year; defeat of the **Motion of Thanks** in the Lok Sabha defeats the government (a Rajya Sabha defeat—as in 1980, 1989, 2001, 2015, 2016—does not).

### Question Hour, Zero Hour, motions and resolutions

**Question Hour** (first hour in LS; 12–1 pm in RS since 2014) has four types: **Starred** (asterisk, green—**oral + supplementaries**), **Unstarred** (white—**written, no supplementaries**), **Short Notice** (pink—**<10 days' notice, oral**), and **Questions to Private Members** (yellow—non-minister MPs on Bills/business in their charge). **Zero Hour** is an **Indian innovation (1962)**, **NOT in the Constitution or Rules**, letting MPs raise urgent issues informally right after Question Hour (11 am in RS).

Motions are **Substantive** (self-contained, e.g., impeachment/removal), **Substitute** (alternative superseding the original), or **Subsidiary** (*Ancillary, Superseding, Amendment*). Key devices:
- **Closure Motions (4 kinds):** *Simple* (sufficiently discussed, vote now); *By Compartments* (clauses voted by part); *Kangaroo* (only key clauses debated/voted; intervening skipped as passed); *Guillotine* (undiscussed clauses or **Demands for Grants** voted wholesale when time ends).
- **Privilege Motion:** Censures a minister for withholding or distorting facts.
- **Calling Attention Motion:** **Indian innovation (1954)** that—**unlike Zero Hour**—**IS in the Rules**; elicits an urgent ministerial statement without vote/censure.
- **Adjournment Motion:** **Lok Sabha ONLY** (element of **censure**); needs **50 MPs**, interrupts business for an urgent definite recent matter, lasts **>=2.5 hours** (bars sub judice/privilege issues).
- **No-Confidence vs Censure Motion:**

| Feature | No-Confidence Motion | Censure Motion |
|---|---|---|
| **Basis & House** | **Art 75(3)** + **LS Rule 198** (phrase *not* in Constitution); **LS ONLY** | Lok Sabha Rules; **LS ONLY** |
| **Reasons?** | **No reasons needed** | **MUST state specific reasons** |
| **Target & threshold** | **Entire CoM ONLY**; **50 MPs** | Individual, group, or whole CoM; no 50-MP floor |
| **Effect of passage** | Entire ministry **must resign** | Does **NOT legally force resignation** |

- **Other devices:** **Confidence Motion** (government proves majority; defeat forces resignation); **No-Day-Yet-Named Motion** (admitted, date unfixed); **Dilatory Motion** (delays debate); **Point of Order** (breach of rules—**suspends proceedings**, **no debate**, Chair's ruling final); **Half-an-Hour Discussion** and **Short Duration Discussion** (LS **Rule 193** / RS **Rule 176**, **2 hours**)—both have **no motion or vote**; **Special Mention (RS)** / **Rule 377 (LS)**.
- **Resolutions (Private Member's, Government, Statutory):** **Every resolution is a substantive motion and MUST be voted upon** (unlike motions); cannot be withdrawn without leave of the House.

## 5. Impartiality of the Chair and Opposition space: Mains analysis

In Britain, the Commons Speaker **resigns from their party**, contests unopposed (*"Once a Speaker, always a Speaker"*), and enters the House of Lords on retirement. In India, though **Tenth Schedule Paragraph 5** shields presiding officers who quit their party on election, almost no Speaker since **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy (1967)** has resigned party membership—fuelling partisan flashpoints over Money Bills, delayed defection rulings and Opposition suspensions.

Reforms converge on four steps: (1) adopting the **V.S. Page Committee (1967)** norm of uncontested return plus cooling-off bars on post-retirement political posts; (2) shifting **Tenth Schedule** adjudication to the President/Governor on ECI advice (**2nd ARC, 2007**; **Law Commission 255th Report, 2015**) or an independent judicial tribunal (***Keisham Meghachandra Singh*, 2020**; *Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992); (3) promptly filling the **Deputy Speaker** post (Opposition convention); and (4) institutionalising UK-style **Opposition Business Days**, shielding Question Hour, and adopting **NCRWC (2002)** sitting-day floors (120 LS, 100 RS).

> **Mains angle:** Contrast **UK Speaker neutrality** with India's unused **Tenth Schedule Para 5**, cite 2024–2026 evidence (seven-year Deputy Speaker vacancy; June 2024 contested poll; March 2026 removal motion), and close with **Page Committee**, **2nd ARC** and ***Keisham Meghachandra Singh* (2020)**.

- **Parliament (Art 79):** **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha**; President is integral without being a member.
- **Rajya Sabha (Art 80):** Max **250**; current **245** (225 States + **8 UTs**: J&K 4 [Oct 2025], Delhi 3, Puducherry 1 + 12 nominated). Population-based (**4th Schedule**); PR-STV + **open ballot** (*Kuldip Nayar*, 2006); **no NOTA** (*Shailesh Manubhai Parmar*, 2018).
- **Nominations trap:** Art 80(3) lists **4 fields** (literature, science, art, social service)—**NO cooperative movement** (MLCs only, Art 171(5)).
- **Lok Sabha (Arts 81–82):** Max **550** (530 + 20; **104th Amdt, 2019** ended 2 Anglo-Indian seats); current **543** (524 States + 19 UTs; **84 SC + 47 ST = 131** till 2030). State totals frozen at **1971 Census** till post-2026 (**84th Amdt**); internal redrawing on **2001 Census** (**87th Amdt**); <60 lakh exempt.
- **2026 updates:** **106th Amdt** (33% women's quota in LS/Assemblies/Delhi, not RS/Councils) notified **16 April 2026**, linked via Art 334A to **Census 2027**. **131st Amdt Bill, 2026** (850 LS seats, 2011 delimitation) **defeated 17 April 2026 (298–230)**. **129th Amdt Bill, 2024** (One Nation, One Election) with JPC till Winter 2026.
- **Duration (Art 83):** RS permanent (1/3rd every 2 yrs); **6-yr term is in RPA 1951, NOT Constitution**. LS 5 yrs; Emergency extension **1 yr at a time**, max **6 months** post-Emergency.
- **Qualifications & disqualifications:** Age **30 (RS) / 25 (LS)**; elector anywhere in India. **Office of profit** undefined (*Jaya Bachchan*, 2006; Ministers exempt under **Explanation to Art 102(1)**). ***Lily Thomas* (2013)** struck down s.8(4) RPA 1951 (instant disqualification on >=2-yr conviction). **Art 103** = President on **binding ECI advice**; **10th Schedule** = Chair.
- **Vacation (Art 101):** Both Houses → **10 days** (else **RS** lapses); Parliament + State → **14 days** (else **Parliament** lapses); 2 seats same House → **14 days** (else **both**); resignation rejectable if not voluntary (**33rd Amdt, 1974**); **60-day** absence (excluding prorogation / >4-day adjournment).
- **Art 88 & Oath:** Ministers & **AG** speak in either House without voting where non-members. Oath (**Art 99**); ₹500/day penalty (**Art 104**).
- **Speaker (Arts 93–96):** Election date by **President** (**Om Birla**, re-elected 26 June 2024 vs K. Suresh); **survives LS dissolution**; resigns to Deputy Speaker; **casting vote (Art 100)**; Money Bills; Joint Sittings; chairs BAC/Rules/General Purposes.
- **Speaker removal:** **Effective majority** + **14-day notice**; cannot preside (Art 96), **votes in first instance** (no casting vote). Motion vs Om Birla **defeated 11 March 2026** (Jagdambika Pal presided).
- **Deputy Speaker, Panel & pro tem:** Date by **Speaker**; auto-chairs committees joined; **vacant since May 2019**. **Panel** (max 10) presides ONLY on **absence**, NEVER **vacancy** (President appoints, Art 95(1)). **Pro tem** by President (Bhartruhari Mahtab, June 2024).
- **RS Chair (Arts 89–92):** VP **C.P. Radhakrishnan** (12 Sept 2025) is ex-officio Chairman (not RS member; **no vote** on own removal; no Money Bill/Joint Sitting role). **Harivansh Narayan Singh** re-elected **17 April 2026** (3rd term; **first nominated MP** as RS Deputy Chairman).
- **Leaders, Whip & Secretariat:** **LoP** statutory (**1977 Act**, Cabinet rank, 10% convention; **Rahul Gandhi** LS, **Mallikarjun Kharge** RS). **Whip** = **convention only**. **Art 98** = independent Secretariats.
- **Sessions & endings (Art 85):** Max **6-month** gap; Adjournment/*sine die* = **Chair**; Prorogation/Dissolution = **President**. **Prorogation does NOT lapse Bills**. **Quorum (Art 100(3)):** **1/10th including Chair** (LS 55, RS 25).
- **Special Address (Art 87):** Post-election and annual; defeat of **Motion of Thanks** in LS defeats government.
- **Questions & Zero Hour:** Starred (oral + supplementaries), Unstarred (written), Short Notice (<10 days, oral), Private Member. **Zero Hour** (1962) **NOT in Rules**; **Calling Attention** (1954) **IN Rules**.
- **Closure & Floor Devices:** Simple, Compartments, **Kangaroo**, **Guillotine**; Point of Order (**no debate**); Half-an-Hour & Rule 193 (2 hrs, **no vote**); **every resolution is a substantive motion and MUST be voted upon**.
- **LS-only censure tools:** **Adjournment** (50 MPs, >=2.5 hrs); **No-Confidence** (Art 75(3)/Rule 198, 50 MPs, **no reasons**, whole CoM, forces resignation); **Censure** (**reasons required**, individual/group/whole CoM, no legal resignation).
- **Mains reforms:** UK Speaker vs **Tenth Schedule Para 5**; **Page Committee (1967)**, **2nd ARC**, **Law Commission 255th**, ***Keisham Meghachandra Singh* (2020)**, Opposition Business Days & NCRWC sitting targets.
