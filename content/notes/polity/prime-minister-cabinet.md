## 1. The Prime Minister: pivot of India's parliamentary executive

In India's parliamentary system, the President is the head of the State, whereas the **Prime Minister** is the head of the Government—the real (*de facto*) executive authority around whom the entire Union administration turns.

> "If any functionary under our Constitution is to be compared with the U.S. President, he is the Prime Minister and not the President of the Union." — B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly

### Appointment, eligibility, oath and term

The Constitution lays down **no specific procedure** for choosing the Prime Minister. **Article 75(1)** states simply that the President appoints the Prime Minister, and other ministers on the Prime Minister's advice. By parliamentary convention, the President invites the leader of the majority party or largest pre-poll coalition in the Lok Sabha. Situational discretion arises only in a **hung Lok Sabha** or upon a Prime Minister's sudden death with no clear successor: President Neelam Sanjiva Reddy appointed Charan Singh (1979) subject to proving a majority, and Presidents R. Venkataraman and K.R. Narayanan institutionalised requiring letters of support and a time-bound **floor test**.

Unlike Britain—where the Prime Minister sits in the House of Commons—India's Prime Minister may belong to **either House of Parliament** (Indira Gandhi 1966, H.D. Deve Gowda 1996, and Manmohan Singh 2004, 2009 sat in the Rajya Sabha). Under **Article 75(5)**, even a **non-MP** may be appointed PM or Minister if they enter either House within **six months** (upheld in ***Har Sharan Verma*, 1971** and ***S.P. Anand v. H.D. Deve Gowda*, 1996**), though ***S.R. Chaudhuri* (2001)** barred repeated six-month reappointments without election. The President administers the oaths of **office and secrecy** (**Third Schedule**). Though ministers hold office "during the pleasure of the President" (**Article 75(2)**), the PM stays in office so long as the government commands **Lok Sabha confidence**.

### Powers, functions and scholarly perspectives

- **Council of Ministers:** Recommends appointments, allocates and reshuffles portfolios, presides over Cabinet meetings, and may ask a minister to resign or advise dismissal. **The PM's death or resignation automatically dissolves the entire Council of Ministers**, whereas another minister's exit merely creates a vacancy.
- **President (Article 78):** Principal communication channel, duty-bound to (a) communicate all CoM decisions on Union administration and legislation, (b) furnish administrative or legislative information the President calls for, and (c) place before the full Council any decision taken individually by a minister if the President requires. Advises on top constitutional appointments (AG, CAG, UPSC, ECs, Finance Commission).
- **Parliament & ex-officio bodies:** Advises on summoning, proroguing and dissolving the Lok Sabha; announces key policies; and chairs **NITI Aayog**, **National Development Council (NDC)**, **Inter-State Council**, **National Integration Council**, **National Water Resources Council**, **NDMA**, and the Political Council of the **Nuclear Command Authority**.

Scholars capture this primacy vividly: Lord Morley called the PM *primus inter pares* ("first among equals") and the "keystone of the Cabinet arch"; Sir William Vernon Harcourt said "a moon among lesser stars"; Ivor Jennings saw "a sun around which planets revolve"; and Harold Laski noted the PM is central to the Cabinet's formation, life and death. Political arithmetic determines practice—strong single-party majorities (Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Narendra Modi) lean toward "Prime Ministerial government", while coalition eras (1989–2014) enforce collegial bargaining.

## 2. The Council of Ministers and the Cabinet

### Constitutional framework (Articles 74, 75, 77, 78, 88)

**Article 74(1)** mandates a Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister to aid and advise the President. Made explicitly binding by the **42nd Amendment (1976)** and tempered with a one-time reconsideration proviso by the **44th Amendment (1978)**, ministerial advice is insulated from judicial scrutiny by **Article 74(2)**: *no court may inquire into whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by ministers to the President*.

To curb jumbo ministries forged to purchase political support, the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** inserted two vital clauses:

- **Article 75(1A):** The total number of Ministers—**including the Prime Minister**—in the Council of Ministers **shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha** (note: Lok Sabha only, not both Houses!).
- **Article 75(1B):** An MP disqualified on grounds of defection under the **Tenth Schedule** is also disqualified from being appointed a minister until re-elected or until their term expires.

Under **Article 88**, every minister (and the Attorney General) has the right to speak and participate in the proceedings of **either House**, any joint sitting, and any parliamentary committee of which they are named a member—**but may vote only in the House of which they are a member**.

### Three doctrines of ministerial responsibility

1. **Collective responsibility (Article 75(3)):** The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible **specifically to the Lok Sabha** (not Parliament as a whole). Ministers "swim and sink together": a Lok Sabha no-confidence vote removes the entire ministry, including Rajya Sabha ministers. Dissenting ministers must resign (e.g., B.R. Ambedkar 1951 on the Hindu Code Bill, C.D. Deshmukh 1956 on states reorganisation, Arif Mohammad Khan 1986 on Shah Bano). In ***U.N.R. Rao v. Indira Gandhi* (1971)**, the Supreme Court held Article 74(1) remains mandatory even after Lok Sabha dissolution—a caretaker ministry continues advising the President until a new ministry assumes office.
2. **Individual responsibility (Article 75(2)):** Ministers hold office during the President's pleasure, exercised on the PM's advice, allowing dismissal of an underperforming or dissenting minister without toppling the government.
3. **No legal responsibility:** Unlike Britain—where ministers countersign royal acts and bear legal liability—Indian ministers do not countersign Presidential orders, and Article 74(2) bars courts from probing ministerial advice.

> **In the news:** **Constitution (130th Amendment) Bill, 2025.** Introduced on **20 August 2025** (with companion Bills for J&K and UTs), the Bill seeks to amend **Articles 75, 164 and 239AA** (inserting Art 75(5A)) to mandate removal of a Minister—or resignation of a PM or CM—detained in custody for **30 consecutive days** on an allegation punishable with **five years or more** imprisonment, with automatic cessation from day 31 (reappointment allowed on release). Referred to a **Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC)** led by Aparajita Sarangi, it remains **pending as of November 2026** after the JPC deferred adopting its draft report in July 2026 and received an extension until Winter Session 2026. The debate weighs **public probity** against **presumption of innocence**, federalism, and potential misuse of pre-trial detention without conviction.

### Three tiers of ministers and the Cabinet vs Council of Ministers

Practice divides the Council into three tiers: **Cabinet Ministers** (head major ministries, attend Cabinet meetings by right, shape national policy), **Ministers of State (MoS)** (either **Independent Charge**, invited to Cabinet only for their subjects, or attached to a Cabinet Minister), and **Deputy Ministers** (junior assistants without independent charge; rarely appointed today). Separate **Parliamentary Secretaries** hold no department and are appointed by the PM rather than the President—now largely disused at the Centre.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Council of Ministers (≤ 15% of Lok Sabha, Art 75(1A))  │
│  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │ Union Cabinet (Cabinet Ministers, Art 352(3))    │  │
│  │  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐  │  │
│  │  │ Prime Minister + 8 Standing Cabinet Panels │  │  │
│  │  └────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │  │
│  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Dimension | Council of Ministers | Union Cabinet |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitutional status** | Original body (**Articles 74–75**) | Word **"Cabinet"** inserted **only once**, in **Article 352(3)** by the **44th Amendment (1978)** |
| **Size & composition** | Wider (~60–80): Cabinet + MoS + Deputy Ministers | Inner core (~15–30): PM + Cabinet Ministers only |
| **Meetings & role** | Rarely meets collectively; executes Cabinet policy | Meets regularly; supreme policy-making & coordinating body |
| **Responsibility** | Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha (Art 75(3)) | Enforces collective responsibility across the Council |

Alongside the Cabinet often sits an informal **"Kitchen Cabinet" (Inner Cabinet)** of trusted colleagues and close confidants—aiding rapid, confidential decisions but criticised for bypassing formal Cabinet collegiality.

## 3. Cabinet Committees

Cabinet Committees are **extra-constitutional**—not mentioned in the Constitution, but established under **Rule 6 of the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961** framed under **Article 77(3)**. They are either **Standing** (permanent) or **Ad hoc** (temporary panels, including **Groups of Ministers**). The Prime Minister constitutes and modifies them; **non-Cabinet Ministers** (e.g., MoS Independent Charge) may serve as members or special invitees; if the PM sits on a panel, the PM invariably chairs it. Committees take **final decisions**, though the **full Cabinet may review** any decision.

Reconstituted on **3 July 2024** after the general election, **eight Standing Cabinet Committees** operate as of November 2026:

| Standing Cabinet Committee | Chairperson (Nov 2026) | Core Mandate |
|---|---|---|
| **Political Affairs (CCPA)** | **Prime Minister** | "Super-Cabinet"; domestic & foreign policy |
| **Economic Affairs (CCEA)** | **Prime Minister** | Economic policy, MSPs, pricing, investments & disinvestment |
| **Appointments (ACC)** | **Prime Minister** | Top civil, military & PSU appointments (**2 members only since 2014**: PM & Home Minister) |
| **Security (CCS)** | **Prime Minister** | Defence, internal security & atomic energy (PM + Defence, Home, Finance, EAM) |
| **Investment and Growth** | **Prime Minister** | Created 2019; fast-tracks major infrastructure & investment projects |
| **Skill, Employment and Livelihood** | **Prime Minister** | Created 2019 as *Employment and Skill Development*; **renamed July 2024** |
| **Accommodation** | **Home Minister** (Amit Shah) | Allotment of government accommodation |
| **Parliamentary Affairs** | **Defence Minister** (Rajnath Singh) | Schedules Parliament sessions & coordinates legislative business |

> **Prelims pointer:** Six panels are chaired by the PM; the **two exceptions** are **Accommodation (Home Minister)** and **Parliamentary Affairs (Defence Minister)**. The Home Minister sits on all eight panels as reconstituted in July 2024. Watch the July 2024 renaming to **Skill, Employment and Livelihood**.

## 4. Cabinet Secretariat and the Prime Minister's Office (PMO)

### The Cabinet Secretariat

Placed directly under the Prime Minister, the **Cabinet Secretariat** is headed administratively by the **Cabinet Secretary**—India's senior-most civil servant (**11th** in the Order of Precedence) and ex-officio Chair of the **Civil Services Board** and the **National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC)** (**Dr. T.V. Somanathan** since 30 August 2024, extended by one year in August 2026). It serves as the neutral nerve centre of the Cabinet: preparing agendas and tracking decisions for the Cabinet and its Committees, resolving inter-ministerial deadlocks via **Committees of Secretaries**, coordinating crisis management, and administering the **Allocation of Business Rules** and **Transaction of Business Rules (1961)**.

### The Prime Minister's Office (PMO)

The **PMO** is an **extra-constitutional** staff agency (created in 1947 as the Prime Minister's Secretariat, renamed **PMO in 1977** under Morarji Desai, and given departmental status under the Allocation of Business Rules). Headed politically by the PM (with an MoS in the PMO) and administratively by the **Principal Secretary to the PM** (Cabinet Minister rank since 2019; as of November 2026, Dr. P.K. Mishra serves alongside Shaktikanta Das as Principal Secretary-2), it acts as the PM's strategic think-tank—monitoring flagship programmes (e.g., **PRAGATI**), liaising with ministries, states, Parliament and foreign governments, and handling grievances and unassigned subjects.

> **Mains angle:** **The "PMO-isation" debate.** Growing centralisation inside the PMO (traced from Indira Gandhi's P.N. Haksar era onward) **breaks departmental silos** and accelerates "whole-of-government" execution, but risks **sidelining line ministers**, eroding Cabinet collegiality, diluting the Cabinet Secretary's mediating role, and creating upward bottlenecks. The right balance pairs strategic steering at the PMO with neutral coordination at the Cabinet Secretariat and operational autonomy inside ministries.

## 5. Central Secretariat, ministries, departments and reform

The **Central Secretariat** comprises all Union ministries and departments (50+ ministries and ~90 departments) created by the President on the PM's advice under the **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961** (Article 77(3)). A ministry may span several departments (e.g., Finance houses six departments: Economic Affairs, Expenditure, Revenue, Financial Services, DIPAM, and Public Enterprises).

```text
   Political Head: Cabinet Minister / MoS (Ind. Charge)
                            ↓
    Administrative Head: Secretary to Government of India
                            ↓
        Wing: Special / Additional / Joint Secretary
                            ↓
         Division: Director / Deputy Secretary
                            ↓
                 Branch: Under Secretary
                            ↓
                Section: Section Officer
                            ↓
     Attached Offices (technical/executive direction)
                            ↓
       Subordinate Offices (field implementation)
```

Three operational layers divide policy from delivery:

- **Secretariat (Ministry/Department):** **Staff agency** handling policy formulation, legislative drafting, budgeting, parliamentary accountability and inter-ministerial coordination.
- **Attached Offices:** Provide **executive direction** and **technical advice** directly under ministries (e.g., CPWD, DGFT, Central Water Commission).
- **Subordinate Offices:** **Field establishments** executing policies on the ground (e.g., regional passport offices, customs/income-tax commissionerates, field inspectorates).

### Second ARC (13th Report): reforming the apex machinery

In its **13th Report**, ***Organisational Structure of Government of India* (2009)**, the **Second ARC** proposed four structural reforms:

1. **Rationalise ministries (20–25 groups):** Apply **subsidiarity** (Union keeps core national tasks, devolving others to States/local bodies) to merge 50+ siloed ministries into **20–25 cohesive ministries** under Coordinating Cabinet Ministers.
2. **Separate policy from execution:** Recast the Allocation of Business Rules around mission statements so ministries focus on policy analysis, budgeting, Parliament work and periodic evaluation, while autonomous **Executive Agencies** (bound by performance MoUs) handle delivery.
3. **Flatten hierarchies:** Ensure **no file crosses more than three levels** before decision, expand the **desk-officer system**, enforce clear delegation, and digitise via **e-Office**.
4. **Strengthen coordination and expertise:** Empower the Cabinet Secretariat, guarantee fixed minimum tenures for secretaries, and induct domain specialists via **lateral entry** alongside continuous capacity building (**Mission Karmayogi** / iGOT).

> **Mains angle:** Pairing decisive prime ministerial leadership with revitalised Cabinet collegiality, parliamentary oversight (DRSCs, Question Hour), and Second ARC's leaner, policy-focused ministries delivers executive agility without weakening democratic accountability.

- **Prime Minister (Arts 74, 75, 78):** Real (*de facto*) head of government; Ambedkar compared the PM—not President—to the US President. Appointed under Art 75(1) with no constitutional selection procedure; situational discretion in hung LS (floor-test convention).
- **Eligibility & tenure:** May sit in **either House** (Indira Gandhi, Deve Gowda, Manmohan Singh) or be a **non-MP for 6 months** (Art 75(5); *Har Sharan Verma*, 1971; *S.P. Anand*, 1996; no repeat non-MP spells—*S.R. Chaudhuri*, 2001). Oaths in **Third Schedule**; PM's death/resignation dissolves the **entire CoM**.
- **Article 78 duties:** Communicate CoM decisions, furnish info sought by President, and refer single-minister decisions to full CoM if President requires.
- **Ex-officio chairmanships:** **NITI Aayog**, NDC, Inter-State Council, National Water Resources Council, National Integration Council, NDMA, and NCA Political Council.
- **Scholarly tags:** Morley (*primus inter pares*, "keystone of the Cabinet arch"); Harcourt ("moon among lesser stars"); Jennings ("sun around which planets revolve"); Laski (central to Cabinet's formation, life, death).
- **Article 74(2):** Courts **cannot inquire** into advice tendered by ministers to the President.
- **91st Amendment (2003):** **Art 75(1A)** caps CoM (including PM) at **15% of Lok Sabha strength**; **Art 75(1B)** bars Tenth Schedule defectors from ministership.
- **Ministerial responsibility:** **Collective** to **Lok Sabha** only (Art 75(3); *U.N.R. Rao*, 1971—caretaker CoM continues after LS dissolution); **Individual** to President on PM's advice (Art 75(2)); **No legal responsibility** (no UK-style countersignature).
- **Article 88:** Ministers (and AG) may speak and participate in **either House** and committees they belong to, but **vote only in their own House**.
- **130th Amendment Bill, 2025:** Introduced 20 Aug 2025 (amends Arts 75, 164, 239AA); mandates removal/resignation of PM/CM/minister detained **30 consecutive days** for **5+ year** offences (automatic cessation day 31; reappointment possible on release). **Pending** before JPC (chair: Aparajita Sarangi) as of Nov 2026.
- **CoM vs Cabinet:** CoM sits in Arts 74–75 originally (Cabinet Ministers, MoS Ind./attached, Deputy Ministers); word **"Cabinet"** appears **only once**, in **Article 352(3)** (**44th Amendment, 1978**).
- **Cabinet Committees (extra-constitutional):** Framed under **Rule 6, Transaction of Business Rules, 1961** (Art 77(3)). Standing vs Ad hoc (GoMs); non-Cabinet ministers allowed; Cabinet may review decisions.
- **8 Standing Committees (reconstituted 3 July 2024):** PM chairs **6**—Political Affairs ("Super-Cabinet"), Economic Affairs, Appointments (PM + Home Minister only since 2014), Security (PM + Defence, Home, Finance, EAM), Investment & Growth, and **Skill, Employment & Livelihood** (renamed July 2024). **2 non-PM chairs:** **Accommodation (Home Minister)** and **Parliamentary Affairs (Defence Minister)**.
- **Cabinet Secretariat:** Under PM; headed by **Cabinet Secretary** (senior-most civil servant, 11th in Precedence, chairs Civil Services Board & NCMC; **Dr. T.V. Somanathan** since Aug 2024, extended Aug 2026); administers Allocation & Transaction of Business Rules (1961).
- **PMO:** Extra-constitutional staff agency (1947 PM Secretariat → renamed **PMO 1977** under Morarji Desai); headed by **Principal Secretary** (Cabinet rank since 2019; Dr. P.K. Mishra & Shaktikanta Das as PS-2; moved to Seva Teerth Feb 2026). Mains debate: **"PMO-isation"**.
- **Central Secretariat & offices:** Created under **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961** (Art 77(3)). Hierarchy: Minister → Secretary → Additional/Joint Secretary → Director/Deputy Secretary → Under Secretary → Section Officer. **Secretariat** (policy) vs **Attached Offices** (executive direction/technical advice) vs **Subordinate Offices** (field execution).
- **Second ARC (13th Report, 2009):** Merge into **20–25 cohesive ministries** (subsidiarity); separate policy from execution via **Executive Agencies**; max **3 file levels** + desk-officer system + e-Office; strengthen Cabinet Secretariat; fixed tenures & **lateral entry**.
